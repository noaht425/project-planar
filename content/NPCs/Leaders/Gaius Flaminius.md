Ninth king of the [[Kingdom of Geno]], and the last before [[The Great Unrest]].

Home Realm: [[Arenis]]

Race: elf and elemental mix

Born: 76 Auctera, 398 AM. King from 65 Mortera, 419 AM. Died: 32 Morcaela, 546 AM.

## How He Got It

[[Iras]] abdicated in 419 AM and gave no reason. The council of Geno elected Flaminius to succeed him.

[[Lux]] had a hand in that election and everyone involved knew it. He had beaten the progenitor [[Genasi]] in 405 AM, kept the Genasi line in [[Pride]], then agreed that someone outside his own line would hold Geno. Flaminius is how that agreement was honoured. The election was real. So was the archdevil's interest in the result.

## The Reign

A hundred and twenty seven years, and he was better at it than anyone expected.

He favoured Lux and Lux's ideals openly. Pride was given a standing in Geno it has nowhere else, the archdevil's clergy were welcome at court, and a man who wanted advancement under Flaminius did well to be seen admiring the right things.

Setting that aside, he governed well. He kept the wall funded, he held the kingdom out of the Krotaphos war without the drift that had cost [[Genasi VII]] his officers, and he left the treasury in better order than he found it. He heard petitions in person, at his predecessor's desk, for over a century.

## The Slander

A great deal has been written against him, during the reign and since. Most of it comes from the same two sources, the people who wanted the throne in 419 and the people who wanted it after 546. It says the same things in both cases: that he was an archdevil's placeman, that his election was bought, that the good years were the officers rather than the king.

Some of that is true. The weight of it is not, and the record will support a fair reading of him by anyone willing to do the reading.

## After

He died on 32 Morcaela, 546 AM with no successor chosen. [[The Great Unrest]] began four days later and ran for a hundred and eleven years.

> *I set out to confirm one of the stories about him. He had the petition desk moved to a lower room, the story goes, so that petitioners would not have to climb. It is not true. What is true is that the story has been told both ways, by people quoting the same clerk's journal. Against him, as evidence that he was performing humility for the hells. For him, as evidence that he was decent. The journal says he moved it because the upper room was cold.*

## Design Notes

Flaminius is the setting's test case for a compromised institution working anyway. He was genuinely Lux's beneficiary as well as a genuinely good king. A party will meet both versions of him in the sources before it meets the facts.

The propaganda is the useful part. A DM can hand a party a confident account of him from either direction and both will be partly wrong.

## Open

- What Lux actually did to shape the election is unwritten.
- Why he never settled the succession in a hundred and twenty seven years is unwritten, and is the question the Unrest turns on.
