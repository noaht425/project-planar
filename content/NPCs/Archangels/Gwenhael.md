Split from [[Aaranea]] by [[The King of Night]] during [[The Division]]. Archangel of Sacrifice.

Home Realm: [[Iudis]]

Member of the [[Archangels]].

## Appearance

Gwenhael (GWEN-hyle) is a bone-thin man, naked, with both arms occupied in covering himself. He is the only one of the six who appears to be in distress at all times.

He is thinner each time he is seen. Nobody who has recorded him twice has recorded the same figure.

## Manner

[[Aaranea]] meant to love. [[Lilien]] kept the wanting and Gwenhael kept the giving up, and he has nothing left to give that he has not already given.

Sacrifice in his domain is voluntary and it is the only afterlife anyone can enter by choice from another. The blessed there give something up for someone else, repeatedly, and it is a reward because in that realm the person on the other end actually receives it.

Gwenhael will grant almost any request made of him. The cost is taken from him rather than from the petitioner, and he does not mention what it was. Several of the things he no longer has were granted to mortals who did not know they were asking for them.

> *I went to Gwenhael with a small request for this book, a record I had been refused everywhere else, and he said yes before I had finished the sentence. Then he sat down, which I was told he does not do. We talked about nothing for a while. When I stood to go he could not look up. The attendant who showed me out asked me, very quietly, not to come back.*

## Goals

Gwenhael wants the [[Divine War]] never restarted, and his reason is particular: the war was fought over territory, and he gave up his claim in the first year and has never been forgiven for it by the other five.

He opposes the purification of the [[Omens]] and has said so once, in a single line, and has not raised it again. [[Neema]] is his only ally among the six and neither of them has the standing to do anything.

What he is working toward is a petitioner who will refuse him. Nobody has.

## Design Notes

Gwenhael really is generous, which makes him dangerous for a party to deal with more than once. There is no catch. The cost is real, and he pays it.

Sacrifice is the only afterlife a soul can choose to enter, and it is the one place where giving something up reaches the person it was for. Everywhere else in the setting, giving something up is wasted.

A party that has worked out what dealing with him does to him can give him the thing he wants by refusing him, which means walking away from something they need.

## Open

What he has already given away, and what remains, is unwritten and should be decided at the moment a party asks him for something.
