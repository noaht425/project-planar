Two headed serpent. Decides if you are good or bad overall.

Lives in [[Liminia]]. Created after the [[Divine War]].

Member of the [[Nekerux]].

## Appearance

Bis (BISS) is a serpent with two heads. The heads are the same size and neither of them leads.

## Manner

Bis hates neutrality, and this is the operating fact about the whole of [[Liminia]]. A soul that comes to him balanced does not get a balanced verdict. It gets weighed again, and again, and the second head will not agree to a wash.

He has gotten stuck nitpicking the exact morality of tiny actions. A theft of bread forty years before death gets the same scrutiny as a murder, and he is aware that this is happening and considers the alternative worse. He has been slowing down for three thousand years.

[[Themis]] of the [[Archangels|archangels]] reads every decision he makes and sends him an objection whenever she disagrees, which is often. He reads all of them. He has never once replied.

> *I went to Bis on behalf of the family of a printer I had known, to argue that he had been sent to the wrong side. I made the mistake everyone makes in Liminia and said that he had been a decent man on the whole. Both heads stopped. One asked me to set out the whole, item by item, and the other began writing it down, and by evening we had got as far as his sixth birthday.*

## Goals

Bis wants to get it right, which is the entire problem.

He has no stake in the heavens or the hells, no loyalty to either side, and nothing he wants from anyone. What he has is a backlog that he made himself and will not clear by lowering the standard.

He will hear an argument from anyone, including a mortal, including about a soul that was sorted centuries ago. He has reopened cases. This is the single most useful fact in [[Iudis]] for a party with a dead friend in the wrong place.

## Design Notes

Bis is the appeal. The twelve cannot overturn him, which means the only route to moving a soul runs through a two-headed serpent who is willing to listen and almost impossible to finish a conversation with.

His paralysis is, on my reading, partly [[Themis]]'s doing. Three thousand years of written objections from the archangel of justice would do that to a judge, and she did not mean to and has not stopped.

Hating neutrality is the detail that makes him usable rather than merely slow. A party that argues their friend was decent on balance has picked the one defence he will not accept.
