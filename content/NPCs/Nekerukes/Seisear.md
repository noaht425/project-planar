Six headed dog. Decides which of the hells or heavens you go to.

Lives in [[Liminia]]. Created after the [[Divine War]].

Member of the [[Nekerux]].

## Appearance

Seisear (SHESH-ar) is a dog with six heads, one for each of the six portions of [[Iudis]]. The heads are not identical and people who have seen them disagree about which is which.

## Manner

Seisear is fast. [[Bis]] settles whether a soul is good or bad and takes as long as he takes, and then Seisear decides which of the six portions it belongs to, and that decision is made in a moment and is never revisited.

He does not explain. He has never given a reason for a placement and has never been asked for one twice.

He is, by every account I have collected, friendly. Souls arriving at the end of the worst part of the process describe a dog that was pleased to see them.

> *Six heads and one of them had my name before I said it. I was not dead and I want that recorded, because the welcome was the same welcome. Whichever head it was went through the business of being glad I had come, and then the whole animal lost interest in me completely, because I was not sorted and there was nothing for it to do.*

## Goals

Seisear wants nothing. Of every being in this book he is the one I could find no ambition in at all, and I looked.

He does his half of the work, he is good at it, and he has never been late. The twelve have no complaint about him and [[Themis]] has never sent him an objection.

## Design Notes

Seisear is the counterweight to [[Bis]]. One half of the judgement is endlessly arguable and the other half is instant and final, and a party that wants a soul moved between domains rather than between sides has no route at all.

Making him pleasant is deliberate. The friendliest thing in [[Iudis]] is the one with no give in it.

Six heads for six portions is my reading of what he is for, and it fits the pairing of each heaven with the hell most like it. A single head picks the portion and Bis has already picked the side.

## Open

Which head is which portion, and whether a soul can appeal to a different head, is unwritten. The second one is a bad idea mechanically and I have left it alone.
