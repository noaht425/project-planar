Thirty leaves taken from the secret archive of the Temple of [[Brilis]] in [[Kingdom of Geno|Geno]], given to [[Wisp]] by her mentor Gustav on the day he sent her away. Written in [[Theis]], the language of the [[Beings of the Eternal Void]].

The leaves are the Void's own history of the world, from the Kings alone in the dark to the Council of Creation. Nobody has accounted for how they left the Void or how the temple came to hold them.

> *Two hands wrote this book, and the second one went back and slipped a leaf in among the first one's work. The characters change shape partway through and the grammar changes with them, which is how you date the halves without reading a word of the contents. Whoever did it made no attempt to hide the join. I do not think it had occurred to them that anyone outside the Void would ever be holding it.*

## What The Book Covers

- Leaves I to IV are the Kings: the Void, the names others call them by, their domains, their marriage and their children.
- The book never writes either King's name. It calls them dagur and takur, the crowned one of the light and the crowned one of the dark, which are titles. Suri and Dalla appear nowhere in it, and leaf II says so outright: the true name of the King of Day is not in these marks, and the same for the King of Night.
- That is also why those titles can be mistaken for the weather. Dagur sits one letter from dab, the day, and dagurtakur one letter from dabtap, time. A translator who slips there reads a sentence about the Kings as a sentence about the sky.
- Leaf II was added later, by the second hand, and is the only leaf out of place that way. The names on it are the ones mortals and the other outsiders use, so it could not have been written when the leaves around it were, since mortals did not yet exist. Someone went back and put it in with the rest of the King material.
- That is also why those names look the way they do. Pater Diei, Augustus, Aster and Nocturnis are not Theis and never were; they are the names the peoples outside the Void gave the Kings, and the Void wrote them down as foreign sounds between the marks, which is exactly what they are.
- Leaf III lists each King's domains. The King of Day holds five. The King of Night's list has five places in it and only four words. The second place is empty.
- The gap is not damage and not an erasure. The word is simply gone, as though it were lifted off the page, and the sentence that held it is still there around the hole. What is missing is death, taken from [[The King of Night]] by [[Orcus]], but the book does not say so, because the book no longer contains the word.
- A reader can tell that something belongs there. The line runs Hie, a gap, duril, and every other clause on the leaf runs Hie, a word, duril. Nothing in the text says what the word was.
- Leaves V to XII are the plan of the planes, read outward.
- Leaves XIV to XXIV are the history: the Division, the Syzygy, the Divine War, the Great Separation, and what became of each realm afterward.
- Leaves XXVIII to XXX close it: the names outsiders use for the eight realms, the two ages, and the Void's last word to whoever is reading.

## The Two Hands

Leaves I to XIII are in one hand and leaves XIV to XXX in another, with one exception. Leaf II is in the second hand, sitting among the first. The hands differ in the shapes of the characters, and the grammar differs with them, which is what dates the two halves.

- Leaves I, III to XIII use joined words throughout, including Dagurtakur, the two Kings as one. That word has not been spoken in the Void since the Syzygy, so these leaves were written before 3,096 AF.
- Leaf II and leaves XIV to XXX use broken pairs: dagur dagur, takur takur, gud gud. These were written after the parting.
- The hinge falls between leaves XIII and XIV. The first hand stops with Indri whispering to Night, and the second hand opens with the Division itself.

A reader who works out the pairing can date the leaves without translating a word of them. A reader who does not will take dagur dagur for a repeated word and lose the whole point.

## Reading It At The Table

- Every name from outside the Void sits between bar and par. Theis cannot count past one, a pair and all, so wherever another language would give a number the Void writes out every name: the realms, the sphinxes, the winds, the dragons, the Judgements, the fae, the twelve halves they were split into, and the progenitors.
- Eos and Cado appear on leaf IV with no marks around them, because they belong to the Void. Unmarked names among dozens of marked ones are the second foothold a translator finds.
- The sphinxes are a third. Their names are not sounds but descriptions, so Theis writes them as ordinary words rather than enclosing them: Ri ʾazuš, Ri ʾazil, Ri nuʾazil. The third is the Void's way of saying a thing that has not begun, since Theis has no future tense.
- Comprehend Languages and Tongues return fragments rather than sentences. See [[Theis]] for why. A casting on one of the list leaves gives a reader a string of names and nothing that joins them.
- [[Wisp]] has read it once, under a potion, and got the sense of it rather than the words. What she retained is in Wisps Reading.pdf, in this folder. She has no line of the book translated and cannot read a word of Theis.

## What Wisp Has

She read it once, fast, with a potion that was running out, and wrote down what stuck. Wisps Reading.pdf is that document. It holds nothing the players were not already told.

What she retained: the Kings and their children, that the book gives other names for each King and refers to a true name for each without writing it, Cado as the sunset and Eos as the sunrise, the Division and the Syzygy and the Divine War and the Great Separation in that order, the two ages, that no one has heard from the children, that both Kings are permanently injured, that the domains are listed and that something has been lifted out of the King of Night's, what became of each plane in the last chapter, and the eight proper names of the planes.

What she does not have, and the notebook does not mention: the nesting of the realms and the rule behind it; every roster leaf, which is the sphinxes, the winds, the dragons, the Judgements, the fae and the portions of magic; which pseudonyms the book gives; what the domains actually are; the ten seam realms; the nine progenitors by name; and leaves XIII, XVI, XVIII, XXVII and XXX entire.

The last group matters most. Indri whispering to Night, Tiyanak and Indri, the bargain, and the Council of Creation with Ius Primum in it are all dark, and nothing in her notebook points at them. The gaps carry no pattern, because the reason for them is that she read it once and did not hold everything. A player comparing what she has against the book learns only that she needs another potion.

Her dates are hers, not the book's. Theis has no numbers and places everything in sequence, so "about four thousand years ago" and "ends in 50 AF" came from her own schooling in Geno, matched to the order the book gives. The notebook sets her hand apart from the book's sense for exactly this reason.

## Epithets

Every being is named once, on the leaf that lists its kind, and is called by an epithet everywhere else. An epithet is built from ordinary Theis words, so it takes no marks. Only names from outside the Void sit between bar and par.

The effect is that the list leaves are full of bracketed names and the rest of the book has almost none. Outside those leaves, every bracketed name left in the text belongs to a realm. Leaf XIV, the one about Tiyanak and Indri, contains no bracketed name at all. A reader who cracks the marks gets the rosters and not the plot.

## The Leaves

## Notes for the DM

- Leaf XVI is the dangerous one. It records that [[Tiyanak]] holds [[Indri]]'s name and took his memory, that Indri did not die, and that the other fae do not know. The Void wrote it down because the Void writes everything down. Cut this leaf if the table should not have it yet.
- Leaf XIV records the Division in the word ḫal. Ḫal is a still word, which means it cannot be halved and cannot be softened. The Void chose the one word in its language that leaves no room to argue about what was done.
- Leaf XIII gives Indri's stated reason and then his real one, in that order, with the word muʾaz (to lie) between them. He speaks in the night form of the verb, which is the form for anything said out of sight.
- The five first dragons are on leaf VIII with what each holds. The five made afterward are on leaf IX with whoever gave hands to make them, and the record notes that Okzu and Zaros hold the assisted ones to be lesser.
- Leaf X gives each Judgement by name, by what it was called, and by the pair it held. Leaf XIV splits each pair into the archangel and the archdevil it became, following the [[Original Judgements]] notes. The two leaves are written to be read against each other, and leaf XIV closes by recording that each half now stands as far from its other half as the ring allows.
- The epithets are the Void's renderings of the names in the vault: Aaranea the Lover is žuram, the one who loves; Baator the Xenia is param, the one who takes others in; Charios the Entertainer is žazam, the one who makes laughter; Hesit the Empath is bagpak duram, the one who holds another's feeling; Sylas the Poet is zadam, the one who sings; Themati the King of Kings is ziʾuramak ziʾuram, the ruler of rulers.
- Leaf XVI records the bargain without endorsing it. Tiyanak gives away the greater part of the realm, keeps the lesser part because Indri is in it, and the Void writes plainly that he did not want peace and that he hid what is known. The quarrel ends anyway, in the next line.
- The five elemental realms are inside Tarkis from the start, on leaf VIII, one to each dragon: the fire to Pyrrha, the water to Vetur, the wind to Zaros, the stone to Tenu, the rot to Okzu. Vetur's cold and Zaros's lightning are given as what each holds within their realm.
- Leaves XX and XXI are one sequence. Tarkis breaks and the five realms do not; they drift apart, and a seam opens between every pair of them. A lesser realm begins at each seam, and the Void lists all of them, which comes to ten because five realms make ten pairs. The list is set out as a descending block, four lines then three then two then one, because the Void writes every pair once and in order.
- Each seam realm is named on its own line, after the two realms that make it: Žudal is steam, Baḫir ash, Girzum magma, Dežum smoke, Zilag ice, Mabud marsh, Gazir acid, Behum dust, Duḫar plague, Galim metal.
- Those ten names are paired words like any other. The day form is the realm, the night form is the stuff in it, so šutal is steam and žudal is the place steam comes from. It is the only part of the language that works that way.
- Ten lines of the same shape, five known words recombining, each one answered by a new word after a mark. It is the most legible block in the book, and a translator who gets nothing else will still come away knowing which word means and.
- Leaf XXI adds only that the pieces of Ventis and of the hells fell into Tarkis and went to the seams. What they did there is not recorded.
- Leaf XXIV is the Coalescence. The Void writes it with the verb kap, which is the night form of the verb for breaking and means to mend, so the portions of magic are described as having healed into one another. The eight portions are named there in Theis: Baram, Dimam, Gankan, Zulam, Balpal, Zalsal, Guzkus and Nuwaram.
- Nothing in the leaves is dated by number, since Theis has no numbers past one, a pair and all. Everything is placed by what happened before it.

## The Rings

Leaves V to XII are the plan of the planes, read outward from the Void. The Void does not count, so it never gives the number of anything. It writes out every name instead, and the lists come out the lengths they come out.

| Leaf | Realm | What it holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | the Void | the light and the shadow |
| VI | Temporalis | the three sphinxes |
| VII | Ventis | the four winds |
| VIII | Tarkis | the five realms and the five dragons that hold them |
| X | Iudis | the six Judgements |
| XI | Iris | the seven portions |
| XII | Arcanis | portions beyond naming |
| XXV | Arenis | one portion more than Arcanis |

- Leaf V states the nesting and the rule behind it: each realm holds the portions of the realm within it, and one portion more. Theis can say one more, since one is a number it has.
- Leaf V then records that Arcanis does not keep the law, because before the Coalescence its portions could not be named. Leaf XXIV records that it came into line, and names the eight. The cosmos corrects itself partway through the book.
- A reader who counts the lists finds the sequence without a single number being written. That is the whole point of the arrangement, and it is the one discovery in the book that needs no translation at all.
- The winds are given by what each holds rather than by a compass: the cold, the rising of the light, the warmth, the falling of the light. Theis has no words for the directions, so it builds them out of what the Kings do.
- Leaf XI records which fey portions stand opposite one another: the seed against the rot, the finding against the bone, the riddles against time. The growing and the dying stands alone, since nothing faces it.
