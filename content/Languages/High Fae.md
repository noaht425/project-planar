Language of the [[Archfey]] and the fae of [[Iris]]. Parent of [[Sylvan]], and through Middle High Fae a source of [[Low Fae]].

> *In High Fae a use-name is always softened when spoken. Say it hard and you are claiming it is the person's true name, which is a threat, and the fae do not treat it as a slip of the tongue. I have never been so careful about a consonant in my life.*

## Grammar: Sounds
- High Fae uses the letters of Common. An acute accent marks a long vowel: á, é, í, ó, ú.
- Stress falls on the first syllable.
- Every consonant is broad or slender. A consonant next to a, o or u is broad. A consonant next to e or i is slender, said with the tongue raised toward the roof of the mouth.
- The vowels on both sides of a consonant must agree. Where they would not, an extra vowel is written to make them agree, and it is barely sounded. Lainis is spelled with ai so that the n reads as slender on both sides.
- W appears only in a few very old words, such as Weilas.

| Letters | Sound |
| --- | --- |
| bh, mh | v, or w next to a, o or u |
| ch | as in loch |
| dh, gh | a soft sound at the back of the throat; silent at the end of a word |
| fh | silent |
| ph | f |
| sh, th | h |

## Grammar: Gender
- Every noun has a usual gender, masculine or feminine, given in its entry.
- A speaker can switch the gender of any noun. Feminine makes a thing near, familiar and alive. Masculine makes it formal, distant or grand.
- Gender shows only through softening. The article an softens a feminine noun, and a feminine noun softens the adjective right after it. No other word agrees.
- Example: An mhuireann chailis (the beautiful sea, spoken of as near and loved). An muireann cailis (the beautiful sea, spoken of as vast and far away).
- A person can be spoken of in either gender. Feminine is used for friends and kin, and masculine for strangers and superiors.
- Iron (iarra) is always masculine.

## Grammar: Mutations
- Two mutations change the first sound of a word: softening and eclipsis. A dash means the sound does not change.
- S is not softened before a consonant other than l, n or r.

- Softening is caused by: the article an before a feminine noun; a feminine noun on the adjective after it; the past tense; Neo (not); Á (used to address someone); the possessives Mi, Tu and Fól; and every spoken use-name.
- Eclipsis is caused by: the article na before a noun in the genitive plural; Ce (question); Ad (bound mood); and the possessives Sne, Sib and Éi.

## Grammar: Nouns
- Nouns fall into three declensions according to how they end. There is no word for "a".
- The genitive shows possession and follows the noun it belongs to. The first noun loses its article: Leine an mórdhaigh (the ruler's house).
- The vocative is used to address someone. It comes after Á and is softened.
- After a number, a noun stays singular: Trean fáelinn (three fae).

1st Declension (ends in a broad consonant): Bileann, tree. The genitive singular makes the last consonant slender. A final -ach becomes -aigh: Théanach, Théanaigh.

## Grammar: The Article
- An is the singular article and na the plural. Both stand before the noun.
- An softens a feminine noun: an fháelinn (the fae), an sielbhín (the elf).
- Na eclipses a noun in the genitive plural: Leine na bhfáelinní (the house of the fae).

## Grammar: Adjectives
- Adjectives follow the noun. In the singular, an adjective after a feminine noun is softened: Fáelinn chailis (a beautiful fae), Bileann cailis (a beautiful tree).
- In the plural an adjective adds -a, or -e after a slender consonant. An adjective that ends in a vowel does not change: Na fáelinní cailise (the beautiful fae).
- An adjective can be joined to the front of a noun to make a new word: aisfáel (archfey) from aisear (high) and fáel.

## Grammar: Names
- Every fae has a true name and a use-name. Whoever holds a true name can command its owner. The use-name is worn over it and can be changed, lent or traded.
- A use-name is always softened when spoken. A fae whose use-name is Breaca is called Bhreaca. A name that begins with a vowel takes h-: Oisne is called h-Oisne.
- An unsoftened name is a true name. To say someone's use-name without softening it is to claim it is their true name, which is taken as a threat.
- Names that begin with l, n or r cannot be softened, so they are never given as use-names.
- Fóla is the pronoun for someone the speaker will not name. It takes the same verb forms as Éis, and has its own possessive (Fól) and its own forms with prepositions.
- Names are spoken of as property, with the verbs congbh (hold), méanaigh (lend), fothlaigh (owe), cluth (steal) and gléigh (claim).
- Congbhaim t'ánamh. (I hold your name.)
- Chluth Fóla m'ánamh. (The one I will not name stole my name.)
- Méaním m'fhallánamh dúthut. (I lend you my use-name.)
- The words for sun and moon, Sóbhuirnis and Dóthailleach, were built around the true names of [[The King of Day]] and [[The King of Night]]. The fae split the sounds apart and buried them in longer words, the way they hide anything they have stolen.

## Grammar: Word Order
- Verb, then subject, then object: D'fhid an fháelinn an sielbhín (The fae saw the elf).
- Particles such as Neo, Ce and Ad come before the verb.
- A pronoun subject is left out when the verb ending shows the person: Fidim an sielbhín (I see the elf). In the 3rd person the pronoun follows the verb: D'fhid Éis an sielbhín (They saw the elf).
- High Fae has no verb for "have". A thing someone has is "at" them: Bídh ánamh acum (I have a name; a name is at me).

## Grammar: Verbs
- Most verbs are plain verbs. Verbs whose stem ends in -igh form their present and future differently.
- The past tense softens the verb. A verb that begins with a vowel or f also takes D' in the past.
- The bound mood is used for oaths and deals. It is Ad followed by the eclipsed future. A fae who says something in the bound mood is held to it. Ad mbronnfaim fáille dúthut (I am bound to give you a ring).
- Neo makes a verb negative and softens it: Neo fhidim (I do not see). In the past, Neor comes before the past form without D': Neor fhid (did not see).
- Ce asks a question and eclipses the verb: Ce bhfidir? (Do you see?). In the past it is Cer: Cer fhid? (Did they see?)
- The command form is the bare stem for one person, and adds -(a)ibh for more than one. An -igh verb drops its -gh: Gabh! Gabhaibh! Ánmhaigh! Ánmhaibh!

- High Fae has two verbs for "be". Es states what something is, for good: Es fáelinn Éis (They are a fae). Bídh states how something is now, or what it is doing: Bídh Éis fuaire (They are cold).
- Es never changes for person. Its past is Bhes, its negative Neos, and its question Ces.
- The fae use Es sparingly about themselves. A fae describing themselves says Bím fáelinn rather than Es fáelinn mó, since a plain statement of what one is comes close to a true name.

## Grammar: Numbers
- High Fae counts in twenties.
- An ordinal adds -is to the number. A broad final consonant turns slender first: Fár, Fáiris (first).

| Fár | 1 | Fáiris |
| Dáith | 2 | Dáithis |
| Trean | 3 | Trinis |
| Cethra | 4 | Cethrais |
| Cúic | 5 | Cúicis |
| Séabh | 6 | Séibhis |
| Seachra | 7 | Seachrais |
| Ochtan | 8 | Ochtainis |
| Naoith | 9 | Naoithis |
| Deichna | 10 | Deichnais |

- 11 to 19 are counted "on ten": Fár fors deichna (11), Cúic fors deichna (15).
- Twenties are counted as multiples of fithe (20): Dáith fithe (40), Trean fithe (60), Cúic fithe (100). Mórfithe is 400, twenty twenties.
- Other numbers are built the same way: Fár fors fithe (21), Deichna fors fithe (30), Deichna fors dáith fithe (50), Naoith fors deichna fors cethra fithe (99).
- A number comes before its noun, and the noun stays singular.

## Grammar: Change
- High Fae changes faster than any other language of immortals. The fae treat words like fashion. A word goes out of style, is traded away in a bargain or is stolen, and another takes its place.
- The High Fae taught to the elves at their creation in 1 AM became [[Sylvan]]. Middle High Fae, the form spoken around the [[Kingdom of Thalamas|Thalamas]] civil war in 105 AM, gave many words to [[Low Fae]]. This note records High Fae as it is spoken now.
