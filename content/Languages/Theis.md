Language of the [[Beings of the Eternal Void]]. It has no relatives. A few of its words passed into other languages, but nothing else did, and no language descends from it.

Theis is the mortal spelling of Teʾiš. The Void holds that the word is older than the pairing, as the names of the Kings are, and that it means the first voice. No one outside the Void has spoken it in living memory. Written Theis survives in records that left the Void by routes no one has accounted for.

> *Every root in Theis has a day form and a night form, and said together they make a third thing that is larger than both. Then there are the still words, which have no night form at all and cannot be halved, which is why the Void swears its oaths in them. An oath in an ordinary word that can be broken by saying half of it, and I would like that fact to sit with you as long as it has sat with me.*

## Grammar: Sounds
- Four vowels: a, e, i, u. A doubled vowel is held twice as long: tiim.
- Consonants: p, b, t, d, k, g, s, z, š (sh), ž (zh), ḫ (said in the throat, as in loch), h, m, n, l, r, w, y, and ʾ (a catch in the throat).
- A syllable is a vowel, a consonant and a vowel, or a consonant and a vowel and a consonant. Theis never begins a syllable with two consonants.
- Stress falls on the first syllable of a word, and on the first syllable of each half of a joined word.
- Words are written in the characters of the Void. Everything in this note is written in mortal letters instead, the way the few scholars who have worked on Theis copy it out.

## Grammar: Pairs
- Every root in Theis has two forms. The day form uses the voiced consonants b, d, g, z and ž. The night form is the same word with those consonants unvoiced to p, t, k, s and š. Nothing else changes.
- The night form is the quieter of the two. It is what the Void speaks when it does not want to be heard, and it is how the language marks anything done out of sight.
- The two forms are not synonyms. The day form means the thing as it stands in the light, and the night form means the thing as it stands in the dark. Gan is to see in the light and kan is to see in the dark.

| Day | Night |
| --- | --- |
| b | p |
| d | t |
| g | k |
| z | s |
| ž | š |

- The two forms spoken together make the joined form, day first, which means the whole thing: gan and kan give gankan, which is sight itself, and so knowledge.
- A joined word is more than its halves added together. Dab is a day and tap is a night, but dabtap is time.
- Abstract things are always said in the joined form. A half form used where the joined form belongs is a broken pair and means something quite different. See Broken Pairs.

## Grammar: Still Words
- A root with none of the paired consonants in it comes out the same by day and by night. These are still words.
- A still word has one form and one meaning. It has no joined form, because it is already whole, and it cannot be broken.
- Ma (water), nam (fate), ʾanam (name), ʾir (eye), nir (child), ḫal (division) and marum (mortal) are still words.
- The Void swears its oaths in still words, since a still word cannot be halved. An oath sworn in a paired word can be broken by speaking half of it.

## Grammar: Broken Pairs
- To speak one half of a pair where the joined form belongs is to say that the thing has lost its other half. This is how Theis says absence, loss and lying.
- A broken pair is doubled, so that a listener knows it was meant: gankan is knowledge, and gan gan is knowledge with its other half missing, which is to say a half-truth or a blind spot.
- Dabtap is time. Dab dab is a day that is not followed by a night.
- Dagurtakur is the two Kings as one. Since the Syzygy, the Void says dagur dagur and takur takur, and both mean the same thing: a King without the other.
- A text full of doubled halves was written after the parting. A text that uses joined forms throughout was written before it.

## Grammar: Nouns
- Nouns take their endings in this order: root, then number, then case.
- There is no article and no gender.
- Endings take no part in the pairing. Only -ta and -da do, because they come from a root.

| Ending | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| -e | the one doing something | dare: the dragon (as the one acting) |
| (none) | the one it is done to | dar: the dragon |
| -ak | the one it belongs to | darak: of the dragon |
| -ra | the one it is given or said to | darra: to the dragon |
| -a | where it is | dara: at the dragon |
| -ta | from, out of | darta: from the dragon |
| -da | with, alongside | darda: with the dragon |
| -še | toward, as far as | darše: toward the dragon |

- Da (with) and ta (from) are the day and night halves of one root. Theis treats standing beside a thing and coming away from it as the same thing seen in two lights.
- Modifiers follow the noun they belong to, and the noun carries the endings: dar gal (a great dragon), darmine gal (the pair of great dragons, as the ones acting).

## Grammar: Seam Realms
- Where two of the realms within [[Tarkis]] meet, a lesser realm stands at the seam. Theis names ten of them, one for every pair the five realms can make.
- Each name is a paired word like any other. The day form is the realm itself. The night form is the stuff that fills it.
- Žudal is the realm where fire and water meet, and šutal is steam. Zilag is the realm where water and wind meet, and silak is ice.
- The ten are Žudal, Baḫir, Girzum, Dežum, Zilag, Mabud, Gazir, Behum, Duḫar and Galim.
- This is the only place in the language where a realm and its substance are the two halves of one word.

## Grammar: Counting
- Theis counts one, a pair, and all. There is nothing in between.

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| aš | one |
| min | a pair |
| ḫa | all of them |

- Min and ḫa attach to the noun: nirmin (two children), nirḫa (all the children).
- To speak of a number of things that is neither one nor a pair nor all, Theis names each one. This is why the records of the Void are lists of names.
- Nuʾir is the verb for counting, and it is used only with these three words. There is no Theis for "seven".

## Grammar: Verbs
- A verb has no tense. The beings of the Void were there before time, and Theis marks only whether a thing stands or has ended.

| Form | Ending | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standing | -il | guzil | lives, is living, has always lived |
| Ended | -uš | guzuš | lived and no longer lives |
| Command | none | guz | live |
| Named | -am | guzam | the one who lives, the living |

- Nothing in a verb shows who is doing it. The person is given by a pronoun or by a noun with -e.
- Nu- joins to the front of the verb to make it negative: nuguzil (does not live).
- The question particle ḫu stands at the end of the sentence: dare guzil ḫu (does the dragon live?).
- A verb in its night form is the same act done unseen. Dazil is to be speaking aloud. Tasil is to be speaking in confidence.

## Grammar: Word Order
- Subject, then object, then verb. The verb is always last.
- Mae dar ganil. (I see the dragon.)
- Suffixes do the work that word order does in other languages, so a writer may put a phrase early for weight. The verb stays at the end.

## Grammar: Pronouns
|  | One | Pair | All |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I, we | ma | mamin | maḫa |
| you | wa | wamin | waḫa |
| that one, they | hi | himin | hiḫa |

- Pronouns are still words. A being is the same by day and by night.
- Mi is who and mu is what. Ne is this and ri is that.
- There is no word for it. A thing that is not a being takes ne or ri, or nothing at all.

## Grammar: Names from Outside
- A name that does not belong to the Void is written between bar and par, the two halves of the root for holding something at arm's length.
- Bar Zarus par. (The one called Zaros.)
- The marks are kept even in speech, and the name between them is not declined. The endings go on par: bar Zarus parak (of the one called Zaros).
- Names from the Void take no marks, because they are words of the language like any other.
- A name is first made to fit Theis before it is enclosed. Theis has no v, o or y, so Vari becomes Wari, Zaros becomes Zarus, and Pyrrha becomes Pirra.
- Scholars who have tried to read Theis have always begun here. The marks repeat, they sit around words that follow none of the language's rules, and a reader who knows one name outside can find it.

## Grammar: Epithets
- The Void gives a being its name once and afterward calls it by what it does.
- An epithet is built from ordinary words. A verb with -am is the one who does the thing: patam, the one who unmakes. A noun before duram is the bearer of it: tuš duram, the bearer of the cold. An adjective can stand alone: bid, the bright one.
- An epithet is a Theis word, so it takes no marks. Only a name from outside is enclosed between bar and par.
- This is why a record can name a being plainly in one place and speak of it for the rest of a text without ever writing its name again.
- An epithet can be held, given and taken in the same way a name can, and the verbs are the same.

## Grammar: The Kings
- The Kings have no names in Theis. Dagur is the crowned one of the light and takur the crowned one of the dark, and both are titles, not names.
- Their true names are not words of Theis. They are written nowhere in the language and the Void does not set them down.
- Dagur and takur are held to be the two oldest words there are, and every other pair in the language was made in their image.
- Dagurtakur, the joined form, means the two as one, and after that the Void itself and everything in it. It has not been spoken since the Syzygy.
- Dab and tap are the ordinary words for a day and a night, and dabtap is time. A reader who takes dagur for dab, or dagurtakur for dabtap, will read a sentence about the Kings as a sentence about the weather. Mortal translators have done it for centuries.

## Grammar: Influence on Other Languages
Theis stands apart from every other language. A few of its words were taken up by beings who heard them spoken in the Void.

| Theis | Meaning | Went to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ḫal | divide, sort into portions | [[Old Iudimian]] Xāl, a Judgement, and with it the negative prefix Xa- |
| ʾanam | name | [[High Fae]] ánamh |
| dagur, takur | the King of Day, the King of Night | titles only. The true names of the Kings belong to no language, and the ones hidden in other tongues were never taken from Theis |

- No language outside the Void took the pairing with it. Every one of them kept the halves as separate words and lost the joined forms.

## Grammar: Reading Theis
- No one alive outside the [[Beings of the Eternal Void]] speaks Theis, and the beings of the Void have no interest in speaking to anyone.
- Comprehend Languages and Tongues give a reader the sense of a passage rather than its words. The pairing is the reason. A joined word means more than its halves, and the magic hands back the halves. A reader working this way gets strings of fragments: light, dark, the one held apart, a name, a name, a name. That is somewhere to start. It is not a translation.
- A translator who wants the words has to do it the slow way, from the enclosed names outward.

## Grammar: Examples
- Mae dar ganil. I see the dragon.
- Mae dar nuganil. I do not see the dragon.
- Dare ma ganuš. The dragon saw me, and no longer does.
- Bar Zarus pare žum gižuš. The one called Zaros gave blood.
- Dagurtakure darḫa zimuš. The Kings named all the dragons.
- Dagur dagur beža ʾazil. One King alone sits on the throne.
- Wae mu gudil ḫu. What do you remember?
- ʾa takur, mae dazil. King of Night, I am speaking.
- Gankan nugižil. Knowledge is not given.
- Gan gan ʾazil. There is only half of knowing here.
