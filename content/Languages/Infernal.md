Language of the [[Archdevils]]. Descended from [[Old Iudimian]], from which it began to split at [[The Division]] in 3,097 AF. It still sounds much like [[Celestial]], but the devils stripped its grammar down and filled it with riddles and mockery of the angels.

> *You cannot speak Infernal neutrally. A great deal of the vocabulary was built to insult the archangels, and the ordinary words for perfectly ordinary things are among them. I asked my tutor for a polite way to say a devil's name aloud in a temple. He said there wasn't one, and that this was the point, and he seemed to enjoy telling me.*

## Grammar: Sounds
Celestial and Infernal share these changes from [[Old Iudimian]], which is why the two still sound alike:
- y became i.
- ph became f. th and ch kept their spelling but became fricatives, as in "thin" and Scottish "loch".
- h at the start of a word was lost.
- Between vowels, p, t, k became b, d, g, and s became z.
- Long vowels became short, except ee and oo, which became ie and uo.

The changes also apply where an ending meets a stem: a p, t, k or s that ends up between two vowels is voiced.

## Grammar: Infernal Sound Changes
After the changes shared with Celestial, Infernal speech grew hasty and clipped:
- A final unstressed e, i or o after a single consonant fell away. The infinitive -ine became -in.
- After the stress, the first unstressed vowel standing alone between two single consonants fell out, unless it was the last vowel: Kīnemine (to move) became Kīnmin, Thēmaleton (king) became Thēmleda.
- s before another consonant became sh: Thēsmia (law) became Thēshmia.

## Grammar: Accents
- The acute (approving), grave (condemning) and macron (no judgement) work as in Old Iudimian.
- Infernal judges words by devilish values. Every approving word of Old Iudimian is condemning in Infernal, and every condemning word is approving: Ábla (cruelty) is approving, Nièmil (kind) is condemning.
- A few words keep or gain an approving accent against that rule: Múshtha (reward), Dóxa (glory), Kórda (heart), Aímra (blood), Kráda (power).

## Grammar: Gender
- Demonic is the only ordinary gender. Every noun takes the Demonic endings, and old Angelic nouns now end in -a: Ōmmon (eye) became Ōmma.
- The Angelic endings survive only as an insult. Put on any noun, they make it soft, pious and ridiculous: Kēfa (head), Kēfon (a pious little head).

## Grammar: Nouns
- Infernal has three cases: nominative, object, and genitive. The object case covers everything the other Old Iudimian cases did, with a preposition when the meaning needs one (see Case Prepositions).
- There is no vocative. A person is addressed with the bare nominative
- In the genitive plural, -iix shortened to -ix and fell together with the singular. Context tells them apart.

## Grammar: Mockery
- The devils name holy things with riddles and insults aimed at the angels.
- Tìflit (bright, holy) shares its root with Tīflik (blind). An angel is Tìfliton, "bright one", with the pejorative ending. Heaven is Tìfedra, "the bright seat".
- Gold is Oūrisa, built on Oūra (urine) for the color.
- Sèmnit (honorable) and Sèmnik (stupid) differ by one sound, as do Sèmnitia (virtue) and Sèmnikia (stupidity).
- Prayer is Ptòcha (begging), a priest is Ptòcheta (beggar), and to worship is Gònemine (to knee).
- The archangels have mocking names drawn from how they look: Pòlommon (Blythe, "many-eyes"), Gìmnosthon (Gwenhael, "naked bone"), Eìdolon (Neema, "mirror-image"), Kìpeton (Pavlas, "the bower"), Tìflon (Themis, "the blind one"), Ìalon (Verita, "glass").
- The Division is Lýsia, "the Freeing".
- The angels' own domains are renamed as faults: kindness is Malàkia (softness), temperance is Dìpsa (thirst), humility is Kìpesia (bowing), justice is Tìfloziga (blind scales), sacrifice is Phthòra (waste), truth is Làlia (tattling), peace is Nàrka (numbness), honor is Kòmpa (bragging) and faith is Tìflia (blindness).
- Heavenly things fare no better: a hymn is Mèkia (bleating), a choir of angels is Poìmna (a flock), a halo is Oùrikirka (a piss-ring), an angel's wings are Ptilòdasta (a feather-duster), holy water is Tìfloura (bright piss), and to bless is Ptỳsemine (to drool on).

## Grammar: Word Order
- Object first, then the verb, then the subject.
- Subject pronouns are dropped unless stressed.

## Grammar: Joining Sounds
- Infernal never inserts e between consonants. Stems, endings, prefixes and suffixes run straight together, and the voicing rule applies only where a sound really falls between two vowels: Ēzin (to be), Ēztor (is).

## Grammar: Adjectives
- Before a noun, an adjective keeps only its front part, which attaches to the noun like a prefix: Dòx- + Zēna = Dòxzēna (glorious Zeus). The closing part is dropped.
- Two or more adjectives stack as prefixes, the most essential one nearest the noun: Mā- + Dòx- + Zēna = Mādoxzēna (great, glorious Zeus). The word has one accent, on the first prefix.
- Used on its own, as in "Zeus is glorious", an adjective keeps its joined form: Dòxik. As a noun it takes the noun endings: Dòxika (a glorious one).
- The comparative adds -ter- to the front part: Dòxter- (more glorious).
- The superlative doubles the last consonant of the front part: Dòxx- (most glorious), Krādd- (most powerful). A front part ending in a vowel doubles the vowel instead: Maā- (greatest).

## Grammar: Compounds and Derivation
- Compounds, the title suffix -ath- and derivation work as in [[Old Iudimian]], without the inserted e.

## Grammar: Verb Categories
- Tense: present, past, future. The past comes from the old aorist.
- Voice: active, passive.
- Mood: indicative, subjunctive, imperative.
- Evidentiality: witnessed, reported. Only the indicative marks it.
- Person: 1st, 2nd, 3rd. Number: singular, plural.
- Aspect, the middle voice, the optative and the inferred evidential are gone.
- Reduplication is gone.
- Where long vowels shortened, some subjunctive endings fell together with the present indicative. Context tells them apart.

## Grammar: Numbers
- Counting is in base 6, as in Old Iudimian, with the number words changed by the sound changes: Oūthir, Hín, Dìx, Tīrse, Tēsor, Pēnko, Iūdel, Ōrme, Mȳrre.
- Two is avoided at all costs, now out of hatred for what the speakers once were. Dìx is used only as a curse. Counting uses Īnadirse.
