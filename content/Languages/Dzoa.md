Language of the [[Beastfolk]], spoken across the [[Kingdom of Sylfaen]]. Not related to any other known language. The official Dzoa name for the empire is Ḥadzoa, "the place of Dzoa". Iudimian has lent it many words in everyday speech, which the Sylfaen government does not accept as proper Dzoa.

> *Eight noun classes, and each one does something different to the word that follows it. Fine. Then I learned that every borrowed word is filed into class two, the outsider class, so a foreign word triggers the harshest change in the language and everyone within earshot hears exactly where it came from. You cannot smuggle anything into Dzoa. It announces you.*

## Grammar: Sounds
Vowels:
- Short: a, e, i, o, u
- w as a vowel (like the oo in "book") and y (like the u in "but")
- Long vowels are written with a circumflex: â, ê, î, ô, û, ŵ, ŷ

Consonants:
- Stops: p, b, t, d, k, g, q (made at the back of the throat)
- Curled-tongue stops and nasal: ṭ, ḍ, ṇ
- Breathy stops: bh, dh
- Ejectives (popped stops): p', t', k', q', tl'
- Affricates: ts, dz, tl
- Rounded stops: kw, qw
- Fricatives: f, v, s, z, sh, th (as in "thin"), dd (as in "this"), ch (as in "loch"), gh (the voiced partner of ch), h
- Throat sounds: ḥ (a deep, hoarse h) and ʿ (a catch made by squeezing the throat)
- Laterals: l, ll (a breathy l, as in Welsh), and the lateral affricates tl and tl'
- Clicks: c (the "tsk" click), x (the side click used to urge a horse), ǃ (a hollow pop)
- Nasals: m, n, ng
- Nasal-stop clusters at the start of a word: mb, nd, ng
- r (rolled), rh (a breathy rolled r), w, y (consonant)

## Grammar: Mouth Types
- Sylfaen law recognizes four mouth types. Dzoa is spelled the same way by all of them, and each type pronounces certain letters in its own way. All four pronunciations are correct.

| Mouth type | Peoples | Pronunciation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lipped | Tabaxi, Harengon, Satyr, Bellu, Triton | Every letter as written. |
| Snouted | Minotaur, Loxodon | b and d next to a nasal sound become m and n. Vowels carry a nasal ring. |
| Beaked | Aarakocra, Kenku, Owlin, Alez | No lip sounds: p, b, m become k', g, ng, and w becomes gw. |
| Long-jawed | Dragonborn, Lizardfolk, Saurus | No lip rounding: o, u and vowel w become a, y, y, and kw, qw become k, q. The clicks c, x, ǃ become the ejectives t', tl', q'. |

- Beastfolk whose mouths fit none of the four types are not recognized. Officials record their pronunciation as unclean, whatever they say.

| People | How they speak |
| --- | --- |
| Tortle | A rigid beak and a slow tongue. Lip sounds change as for the Beaked, and the throat sounds ḥ and ʿ weaken to h and a plain catch. |
| Locathah | Gill-breathing. Voiced sounds lose their voice (b, d, g, z become p, t, k, s), and the throat sounds gurgle into ch. |
| Grung | A wide mouth and a throat pouch. Ejectives become plain stops, and vowels stretch long. |
| Kekrik | Mandibles. Every stop is made as a click, and voicing is lost. |
| Yuan-Ti | A forked tongue. s, z and sh merge into one long hiss, and th, dd become s, z. |

## Grammar: Noun Classes
- Every noun belongs to one of eight classes, marked by a prefix. Classes 7 and 8 have no plural.
- Each class sets off a mutation on the word that follows the noun (see Mutations).

| Class | Meaning | Singular | Plural | Mutation it sets off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | People (beastfolk) | Mo- | Ba- | Soft |
| 2 | Outsiders (Omens, humans, elves and other non-beastfolk) | ǃa- | ǃi- | Aspirate |
| 3 | Animals | Qa- | Qi- | Hard |
| 4 | Plants, growing things and body parts | Lli- | Llu- | None |
| 5 | Tools and made things | K'i- | Ti- | Click |
| 6 | Places and land | Ḥa- | Ḥu- | Nasal |
| 7 | Substances | ʿU- | (none) | Breathy |
| 8 | Ideas, speech and abstract things | Dhi- | (none) | Throat |

- A prefix loses its final vowel before a stem that begins with a vowel: K'i- + ilu = K'ilu (crown).
- Body parts count as growing things.
- Every Iudimian loanword is placed in class 2, whatever it means (see Registers).

## Grammar: Mutations
- The word directly after a noun changes its first sound according to the noun's class. This covers adjectives, numbers, possessors and the verb that follows a subject.
- Words that already carry a class prefix mutate the prefix itself: Moʿaṭi + moqulu = Moʿaṭi voqulu (a great person).
- A dash means the sound does not change.

| Sound | Soft (1) | Aspirate (2) | Hard (3) | Click (5) | Nasal (6) | Breathy (7) | Throat (8) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| p | b | ph | p' | — | mh | bh | — |
| t | d | th | t' | c | nh | dh | — |
| k | g | ch | k' | x | ngh | gh | q |
| q | gh | ḥ | q' | ǃ | ng | — | — |
| b, bh | v | — | p' | — | m | bh | — |
| d, dh | dd | — | t' | c | n | dh | — |
| g | drops | — | k' | x | ng | gh | gh |
| m | v | — | — | — | — | — | — |
| ts | dz | — | — | c | — | — | — |
| tl | — | — | tl' | x | — | — | — |
| p', t', k', q', tl' | p, t, k, q, tl | — | — | k' becomes x | — | — | — |
| ll, rh | l, r | — | — | — | — | — | — |
| c, x, ǃ | — | — | t', tl', q' | — | nc, nx, nǃ | — | — |

| a vowel | — | h + vowel | — | — | — | — | ʿ + vowel |

- Examples: Qadzu q'aqulu (a big dog), K'iḍaba ximbele (a good book), Ḥuṭaro mado (seven cities), Ti-ʿawi + tl'ena = Tiʿawi tl'ena (two pens, unchanged).
- After a class 5 noun, an animal's prefix Qa- becomes ǃa-, the same as the Outsider prefix: K'iḍaba ǃadzu (the dog's book) sounds like "the book of the outsider dzu". The Office of the Seal has tried and failed to fix this.

## Grammar: Word Order
- Subject, then verb, then object.
- Adjectives, numbers and possessors follow the noun: Ḥaʿumbe Mot'adza (the emperor's house).
- A question ends with Ṭḥa.

## Grammar: Adjectives
- An adjective takes the class prefix of its noun and then the noun's mutation: Ḥaṭaro ḥaqulu (a great city), Mondebe votl'iṇi (a small child).
- Iudimian loan adjectives in street Dzoa never take a prefix (see Registers).

## Grammar: Pronouns
- The 1st and 2nd person pronouns are ʿAṇa (I), Tlu (you), Siḥa (we) and Nḍu (you, plural). They are usually dropped, since the verb shows the person.
- The 3rd person pronoun is the class prefix with -ye: Moye (that person), Baye (those people), ǃaye (that outsider), Qaye (that animal), K'iye (that thing), Ḥaye (that place), ʿUye (that stuff), Dhiye (that idea).

## Grammar: Verbs
- Dzoa has two kinds of verbs, inherited from the different peoples who were made to share one language. Root verbs come from the desert and scaled peoples, and chain verbs from the coast and forest peoples. The Office of the Seal teaches both.
- Every verb takes a subject prefix: nga- (I), wu- (you), sa- (we), na- (you, plural), and for the 3rd person the class prefix of the subject.

Root verbs are built on three consonants placed into vowel patterns:

| Form | Pattern | q-l-m (speak) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present | CaCiC | qalim |
| Past | CiCaC | qilam |
| Future | za- + present | zaqalim |
| Command | uCCuC | uqlum |
| Participle (the thing done) | maCCûC | maqlûm (a word, a thing said) |

- Example: Ngaqalim Dhidzoa (I speak Dzoa). Mot'adza voqilam (the emperor spoke). The verb's prefix mo- takes the soft mutation from the class 1 subject..
- A root verb is negated with La before it: La ngaqalim (I do not speak).

Chain verbs add endings to a stem:

| Form | Ending | ḥamb- (walk) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present | -a | ḥamba |
| Past | -ile | ḥambile |
| Future | za- + -a | zaḥamba |
| Command | -a | ḥamba |
| Negative present | a- before the subject prefix, -i | angaḥambi (I do not walk) |
| Negative past | a- before the subject prefix, -ili | angaḥambili (I did not walk) |

## Grammar: Numbers
- Dzoa counts in base 10: ʿAḥ, Tl'ena, Qwatu, Nḍeli, Xamo, Sitha, Bhado, Ṭiṇu, Llaqwe, Dzeka, with Mbuṭa (100) and Ḥaz (1000).
- Larger numbers put the ten first and join the rest with Ne: Dzeka ne ʿaḥ (11), Dzeka tl'ena (20), Dzeka tl'ena ne xamo (25), Mbuṭa ne dzeka (110).
- Numbers follow the noun and take its mutation.

## Grammar: Registers
- Pure Dzoa is the language of law, schools and the court, policed by the Office of the Seal (Ḥarhoṭo). Street Dzoa is what people speak, full of words picked up from Iudimian through Dawara and the border.
- Every Iudimian loan goes into class 2, the class for outsiders, and takes the ǃa- prefix. Each loan therefore sets off the harsh aspirate mutation on the next word, so foreign words can be heard in a sentence even when they are mispronounced.
- Loan adjectives never take a class prefix, which the Office counts as an error.
- Loan verbs are made with Fa (to do) and a loanword: Ngafa ǃalalia (I gossip).
- Tsi is used at the end of a sentence for "so they say", as in Iudimian.
- The Iudimian Xa- is used as an "un-" prefix in slang, said with the side click x. The Office forbids it.
- Taverns use the Iudimian saying Hin hemer, dix hemeri ("one more can't hurt") untranslated.

## Grammar: Dialects

## Grammar: Place Names
- Chrasil: from chrasa (pine) and ilu (crown), "pine crown".
- Dawara: from the root d-w-r (guard) and ra (high), "the high watch". The capital stands on the border with Krotaphos.
- Plowo: from plo (salt) and wo (mouth), "salt mouth", the port at the mouth of the sea.
- Oinsyl: named for the border of Oinarria and Sylfaen. The name is not Dzoa, and the Office has never accepted it.
- Ḥadzoa: the official Dzoa name for the empire, "the place of Dzoa".
