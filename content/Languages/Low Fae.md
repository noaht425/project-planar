Language of the [[Fae]] of [[Arenis]], spoken in the [[Kingdom of Kumas]]. It continues the speech of [[Kingdom of Thalamas|Thalamas]], with many words borrowed from Middle High Fae after 105 AM.

> *Low Fae has no word for thanks. The closest you can get is Fotlim dutut, which means I owe you, and which is meant literally and recorded accordingly. I thanked a shopkeeper out of habit on my second day and spent the rest of the week working out what I had agreed to.*

- Words borrowed from Middle High Fae keep more of their old spelling. They hold on to vowel pairs such as ai, ao and ea, double letters and w, so a listener can hear which words came from Iris: kalis (pretty) is native, kailis (exquisite) is borrowed.

## Grammar: Sound Changes
Low Fae shares the early changes that turned [[High Fae]] into the speech of [[Kingdom of Thalamas|Thalamas]] (see [[Sylvan]]). After the split in 105 AM it went its own way:
- C became k, except in ch.
- Th became t.
- A final e became i in words of more than one syllable.

## Grammar: Middle High Fae
- After the split, the fae of [[Kingdom of Kumas|Kumas]] turned back toward [[Iris]] and took up the court speech of the archfey. Most of the words they borrowed belong to court life, fashion and pleasure.
- Where a loan sits beside a native word, the loan is the finer one: finra is wine, fionvar is fine wine.
- Several of these words have since gone out of fashion in Iris, so Low Fae keeps forms the archfey no longer use.

## Grammar: Sun and Moon
- The Thalamas words for sun and moon still carried the hidden names of [[The King of Day]] and [[The King of Night]]. Low Fae replaced them with gealroska (the eye of day) and oiroska (the eye of night).

## Grammar: Gender
- Thalamas speech had lost gender. Low Fae speakers brought back part of it in imitation of the court of Iris, using the genders of High Fae.
- A speaker may soften the adjective after a feminine noun to sound refined: Felin chalis (a pretty fae) instead of Felin kalis. It is a matter of style, and many speakers never do it.

| Sound | Softened |
| --- | --- |

| f | dropped; the word takes h- |

| t, d, g, s, l, n, r | no change |

## Grammar: Names
- Low Fae keeps the fae system of true names and use-names. A use-name is softened when spoken, following the table under Gender.
- Since t no longer softens, names beginning with t joined those beginning with d, g, s, l, n and r as names that are never given as use-names.
- Fola is the pronoun for someone the speaker will not name.

## Grammar: Nouns and Word Order
- The article is an in the singular and na in the plural. A noun adds -a after a consonant and -na after a vowel in the plural. The possessor follows the thing possessed.
- Verb, then subject, then object: Dofid an felin an silvin (The fae saw the elf).
- There is no verb for "have": Bi anav akum (I have a name).

## Grammar: Verbs
- The past adds Do to the front of the verb, as in Sylvan. The future adds -f-. The bound mood, Ad with the future, is used for oaths and deals.
- No makes a verb negative, and Ke asks a question: No fidim (I do not see), Ke fidir? (Do you see?)
- Verbs that end in a vowel drop it before an ending, as in Sylvan.

- Os states what something is, and Bi how it is now. Their forms match Sylvan.

## Grammar: Numbers
- Low Fae still counts in twenties, as the fae of Iris do.
- 11 to 19 are counted on ten: Far fors dechna (11). Twenties are multiples of fiti (20): Dath fiti (40), Kuik fiti (100). Morfiti is 400.
- Fairis (first) is borrowed from Middle High Fae fáiris. Other ordinals add -is: Dathis, Treanis.

## Grammar: Thanks
- Low Fae has no word for thanks. The nearest phrase is Fotlim dutut (I owe you), and it is meant literally.

## Grammar: Words About the Elves
- The fae of Kumas mock the words the elves made in their purge. Several Sylvan words have come into Low Fae with insulting meanings: ondra, doma, pteri, esla, troga and nelis.
- Pteri is the usual word for a pretentious elf.

## Grammar: Place Names
- Kumas: from kumas (hollow, a sheltered place under a hill).
- Velanea: from velas (glory) and lanea (castle). The capital was named before the split and keeps its Thalamas spelling.
- Talamas: the Low Fae form of Thalamas, "the bright alliance".
