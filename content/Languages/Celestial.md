> *Fifty-four words in Celestial mean the opposite of what they meant in [[Old Iudimian]], because the archangels turned them over on purpose. There is no rule for which fifty-four. There is a list. I have copied the list out four times and I could not tell you today whether their word for ruin is a compliment.*

## Grammar: Sounds
Celestial and Infernal share these changes from [[Old Iudimian]], which is why the two still sound alike:
- y became i.
- ph became f. th and ch kept their spelling but became fricatives, as in "thin" and Scottish "loch".
- h at the start of a word was lost.
- Between vowels, p, t, k became b, d, g, and s became z.
- Long vowels became short, except ee and oo, which became ie and uo.

The changes also apply where an ending meets a stem: a p, t, k or s that ends up between two vowels is voiced.

## Grammar: Accents
- The acute (approving), grave (condemning) and macron (no judgement) work as in Old Iudimian.
- Celestial judges words by angelic values. Words tied to the devils, such as Nadaìm (devil), Rafeìm (hell) and Oìnra (wine), are condemning. Words tied to the angels, such as Funsér (angel) and Lunkaú (heaven), are approving.
- A few common words carry a deliberately false accent to mislead listeners: Àrdun (bread), Fìlun (friend), Kàllit (fair), Dòssun (glory), Óbelun (coin), Ídun (water).
- Word shape no longer predicts judgement. Many verbs that end in x or a cluster are now approving (see Inversion).

## Grammar: Word Order
- Subject first, then the verb, then the object.
- Subject pronouns are dropped unless stressed, as in Old Iudimian.

## Grammar: Nouns
- Celestial has no gender. The old endings -on and -a are part of the stem, and every noun takes the same endings: Thémun, Thémunu, Thémunis; Ábala, Ábalau, Ábalais.
- Where two vowels meet, they are written together.

## Grammar: Celestial Sound Changes
After the changes shared with Infernal, Celestial went further:
- k before e or i became ts, and g before e or i became z: Kēpha became Tsēfa (head).
- pt and kt became tt, ps became ss, and x inside a word became ss.
- l before another consonant became u, joining the vowel before it: Mòlxa became Moússa.
- An o without the stress became u: Thémon became Thémun.

## Grammar: Negatives
- Bad things are said by negating good ones with Xa-. The negated word takes the condemning accent: Xanièmun (cruelty, literally "unkindness"), Xavèridun (deceit), Xathèmun (tyranny), Xafìlun (enemy).

## Grammar: Back-slang
- Nouns for holy and powerful things have their syllables reversed, so the devils cannot recognize them: Fāna became Nafā (temple), Dīthon became Thundī (god), Kaūlon became Lunkaú (heaven).
- The accent moves with its syllable.
- Sūrint and Dāllend themselves are never reversed. Words built on the same roots are: Unrisū (sun), Nadaū (moon), Relsū (daily), Zemsūrine (to arise), Derizuō (today).

## Grammar: Verbs
- Celestial keeps the full Old Iudimian verb system: every tense, aspect, voice, mood, and evidential, with the same restrictions.
- The endings below have gone through the sound changes, including the Celestial ones. Where a long vowel shortened, some subjunctive endings fell together with the indicative ones, and context tells them apart.
- Reduplication works as in Old Iudimian.

## Grammar: Numbers
- Counting is in base 6, as in Old Iudimian, with the number words changed by the sound changes: Oūthir, Hín, Dìx, Tīrse, Tēsor, Pēnko, Iūdel, Ōrme, Mȳrre.
- Two is avoided at all costs, now out of hatred for what the speakers once were. Dìx is used only as a curse. Counting uses Īnadirse.
