Ancestor of Common, the language spoken across the world. Common is English, and Torren stands to it as Old English does.

[[Minem]] made it. He was given nothing at his creation and built a language for humans out of nothing, and where [[Genasi]] built Genoan to be admired, Minem built Torren to be used. It has two genders rather than five classes, four cases rather than nine, and no feature in it that does not do a job. He wrote down what he settled on and then left it alone. Torren drifted into Common on its own, over a long time, with no one enforcing anything.

Minem did build it twice, in a sense. Alongside the plain register he made a second one for law, record and reckoning, with its own endings for naming abstractions. Common inherited both. This is why the everyday half of Common and its precise half look like two different languages: *dark* and *night* on one side, *justice* and *temperance* on the other. They descend from the same tongue and the same maker.

> *Everyone arrives at Torren expecting it to be easy, on the grounds that Common is easy and Common came from it. Common is easy because Torren was sanded down for three centuries by people who needed to trade with each other. The thing it came from had two registers and expected you to know which one you were in.*

## Grammar: Sounds
- Vowels: a, e, i, o, u, y, and æ, each short or long. A long vowel is written with a bar: ā, ē, ī, ō, ū, ȳ.
- Consonants: p, b, t, d, k, g, f, v, s, z, h, m, n, l, r, w, j, the pairs sh and ch, þ for the sound in thing, and ƕ for the sound written wh in Common.
- Torren has none of the sliding vowels that make early writing hard to read. Minem allowed one vowel to a syllable and held to it.
- Stress falls on the first syllable in the plain register. In the formal register it falls on the syllable before the ending, which is why the two registers wore down so differently.
- Vowel length is the only thing Torren spelling marks that Common spelling lost. Most of what looks arbitrary in Common spelling is a long vowel that stopped being long.

## Grammar: The Two Registers
- The plain register is what people spoke. Short words, one or two syllables, stress at the front.
- The formal register is what Minem built for law, oaths, records, and counting. Longer words, stress late, and a set of endings for turning a word into the name of a thing or a quality.
- The formal endings are the ancestors of Common's -ce, -ance, -ence, -tion, -sion, -ty, -al, -ar, -ile, -ous, -ive, -ure, -ment, and -y.

| Torren ending | Does | Common ending | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -tia | makes a quality a thing | -ce | Iustitia, justice |
| -antia | the same, on a verb | -ance | Temperantia, temperance |
| -tio | makes an act a thing | -tion | Natio, nation |
| -tas | makes a state a thing | -ty | Libertas, liberty |
| -alis | of or belonging to | -al | Legalis, legal |
| -osus | full of | -ous | Gloriosus, glorious |
| -ura | the result of doing | -ure | Natura, nature |
| -mentum | the thing used or made | -ment | Documentum, document |

- A Torren speaker could tell the registers apart without knowing a word, by where the stress sat. A Common speaker still can, which is why the formal half of the language sounds educated.

## Grammar: Gender
- Two genders, animate and inanimate. Minem sorted by whether a thing acts.
- Gender shows only in the article and in the dative. It does nothing else, and Common dropped it without losing anything.
- Beasts are animate. Fire, wind and water are animate, which Minem is recorded as having argued about with himself.

## Grammar: Nouns
- Four cases. Nominative for the one acting, accusative for the one acted on, genitive for whose it is, dative for the one given to or spoken to.
- Most nouns end in -a and decline alike. Minem built one pattern and put nearly everything in it.

| Case | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nominative | Stāna | Stānas |
| Accusative | Stāna | Stānas |
| Genitive | Stānas | Stāna |
| Dative | Stāne | Stānum |

- The genitive singular and the nominative plural are both -as. Common kept both and they are still the same sound: the -s of *stones* and the -s of *stone's* are one Torren ending that split in writing only.
- The dative plural -um is the one ending Common lost outright.

## Grammar: Verbs
- Two kinds. Weak verbs mark the past with an ending in -d or -t. Strong verbs change the root vowel instead.
- Minem built seven strong classes, each with its own vowel series. Every irregular verb in Common is a Torren strong verb that survived.

| Class | Torren present | Torren past | Common |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | rīda | rāda | ride, rode, ridden |
| I | drīva | drāva | drive, drove, driven |
| I | strīva | strāva | strive, strove, striven |
| I | shīna | shāna | shine, shone, shone |
| III | singa | sanga | sing, sang, sung |
| III | drinka | dranka | drink, drank, drunk |
| III | springa | spranga | spring, sprang, sprung |
| III | finda | fūnda | find, found, found |
| III | binda | būnda | bind, bound, bound |
| VII | halda | helda | hold, held, held |
| VI | standa | stōda | stand, stood, stood |

- Weak verbs were the productive kind even in Minem's day, and every verb coined since is weak. This is why Common's irregular verbs are all old and all common.
- The infinitive ends in -an, the present participle in -ende, the past participle in -ana.

## Grammar: Word Order
- Subject, verb, object. Torren is the only made language in the world that is not verb-first, and Minem chose it deliberately, saying that a person should be named before what they did.
- Adjectives come before the noun, as in Common.
- The genitive comes before the noun it belongs to: Kingas hūsa, the king's house. Common still does this and no other case survives to compete with it.

## Grammar: How Torren Became Common
- The changes below are in order, and each one happened everywhere. Together they turn any plain Torren word into its Common descendant, and every entry in this note has been checked against them.

1. An unstressed syllable standing before the stress is lost. Suris gives rise.
2. In a word stressed on the first of two syllables, the second vowel drops when the consonants left behind can stand together. Darak gives dark.
3. A long a rounds. Stāna gives stone, while a long o does not, so Bōka gives book.
4. A short stressed vowel in an open syllable lengthens while a syllable still follows it. Nama gives name.
5. The final unstressed vowel is lost. What remains of it is the silent e of Common, which marks the length of the vowel before it and nothing else.
6. An unstressed vowel in a final closed syllable weakens to the sound written e. Faþar gives father.
7. A before l and another consonant rounds. Kalda gives cold.
8. The long vowels all shift. Written Common still spells them as they were said before the shift, which is why its spelling looks wrong to the people using it.

- The two words that fix the first two changes are Darak and Suris, and both are in the oldest layer of the language.

## Grammar: Suris and Darak
- Suris is the Torren word for day and Darak the word for night. They are the two oldest words in the language and the two that gave Common *rise* and *dark*.
- Suri is the true name of the [[The King of Day|King of Day]]. Dalla is the true name of the [[The King of Night|King of Night]], and Darak is that name with l gone to r, which is among the commonest changes a word can undergo.
- The same two names sit in the words for day and night in [[Old Iudimian]], [[Celestial]], [[Infernal]] and [[Ancient Draconic]]. None of those languages is related to Torren. Torren is Minem's own work and shares no ancestor with any of them.
- [[Theis]] does not contain the names at all, and holds that the ones hidden in other tongues were never taken from it.
- So nothing carried them. They were not inherited, not borrowed, and not taken from the first language. They are simply in the words for day and night in unrelated tongues made at different times by different hands.
- No speaker of Torren knew this, and no speaker of Common knows it. Everyone who says *sunrise* says the name of the King of Day, and everyone who says *dark* says the name of the King of Night.

## Grammar: Reading Torren
- Drop the final -a and you usually have the Common word, or something close to it.
- A bar over a vowel means the vowel was long. Where Common has a silent e, Torren had a vowel there doing real work.
- A word of two syllables with a cluster in the middle has probably lost a vowel: dark was darak, and the a is still there in the spelling of nothing at all.
- Long words ending in -tia, -tio, -tas or -mentum are formal register. They are not borrowings. Minem made them.
- þ is th and ƕ is wh.

| Hē fand þa stāna. | He found the stone. |
| Þa kingas hūsa standa on þa landa. | The king's house stands on the land. |
| Wē drinka þa water. | We drink the water. |
| Sēo singa on þa darak. | She sings in the night. |
| Þa suris rīsa. | The day rises. |
| Ik halda mīna worda. | I hold my word. |
| Iustitia is nama of þa rihta. | Justice is a name for what is right. |
