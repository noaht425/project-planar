The place at the heart of [[Iudis]] where the dead are judged. The six heavens and the six hells branch outward from it, and every soul arrives here first.

## Arrival

A soul arrives without ceremony and without being met. What it finds is a wide place with the roads of the twelve afterlives leading off it, and a queue.

The queue does not move at one speed. A soul that is plainly one thing or the other can be through Liminia in what a living visitor would call minutes. A soul that is harder to settle can be there a great deal longer, and nothing in the place is arranged to make the wait comfortable, because nothing in the place is arranged for the souls at all.

## The Judgement

Two beings do the work, and neither of them is one of the twelve. At the end of the [[Divine War]] neither side would let the other judge the dead, so the sorting was given to two made beings. See [[Nekerux]].

[[Bis]], a two-headed serpent, decides whether a soul was good or bad on balance. He hates neutrality and will not return a balanced verdict, so a soul that comes to him middling is weighed again until he can call it. He has been slowing down for seven centuries and the backlog is his own doing.

[[Seisear]], a six-headed dog, decides which of the twelve the soul goes to. One head for each portion of the realm. The decision is made in a moment, it is never revisited, and he has never given a reason for one.

There is no third option. Nothing in Liminia sends a soul anywhere but to one of the twelve.

## Appeals

Bis will hear an argument from anybody, including the living, including about a soul sorted centuries ago. He has reopened cases. Nothing in the realm can overturn him, which makes him the only route to moving a soul, and finishing a conversation with him is the hard part.

[[Themis]] reads every one of his decisions and sends him an objection whenever she disagrees. He reads all of them. He has never replied. The objections are copied and posted in the galleries of [[Justice]] beside the rulings they concern.

Seisear takes no appeals, has never been sent an objection, and has never been late.

## The Living

The living can reach Liminia and some do, usually on behalf of somebody. Getting in is the easy half. See [[Iudis]].

> *The queue in Liminia does not move at the same speed for everyone. I watched one soul go from the door to Seisear in the time it took another to be asked a third question by Bis. The second one had been a decent person on the whole, which is the one answer Bis will not take.*

## Design Notes

Liminia is the setting's appeal court and the reason a dead friend is a quest rather than an ending. The route exists, it runs through a judge who will genuinely listen, and it is almost impossible to conclude.

The split between the two is the design. One half of the judgement is endlessly arguable. The other is instant and final. A party can therefore move a soul between sides but never between domains.
