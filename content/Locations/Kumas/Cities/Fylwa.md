Fylwa (FIL-wa) is a town of tall, narrow towers among the pine forests of [[Kingdom of Kumas|Kumas]]. A household lives one room to a floor, and most of the town lives vertically.

## The Towers

The towers are timber, built from the pine around them, and they are narrow because the ground between them is left standing as forest. Fylwa has never cleared its woods and has built upward instead, which the town regards as the obvious solution and visitors regard as perverse.

A tower holds several households, one to a floor, reached by a single stair that passes every door.

> *Fylwa's towers are so narrow that visiting someone means climbing past everyone else who lives there. I was invited to dinner at the top of one, and was formally greeted on every landing on the way up, and owed something on each of them.*

## The Climb

The greeting on each landing is not optional and is not empty. [[Low Fae]] has no word for thanks, the nearest phrase means that the speaker owes something, and a formal greeting on the way past somebody's door is an exchange with a small obligation in it.

So nobody in Fylwa visits casually. A climb to the top of a tower costs the visitor a string of small debts to everyone below, which is why business is done at the foot of the stairs wherever possible, and why an invitation to the top floor is a real compliment.

Residents of upper floors are correspondingly particular about whom they invite, and the top floor of a tower is the prestigious one for that reason rather than for the view.

## The Trade

Timber, pitch, and the carpentry that comes of both. Fylwa frames buildings for half of Kumas and sends the rest of its cut down to [[Velanea]].

## The Ground

The forest between the towers is common land and is not built on, which the town enforces strictly. Pigs run in it, children are sent into it, and a proposal to clear a stretch for building comes up about once a generation and has never passed.

## Design Notes

The stairs are the mechanic. Any scene in Fylwa that involves getting to somebody has a cost attached that has nothing to do with difficulty, and a party that climbs carelessly arrives owing things to strangers.
