Iudis is the realm of divinity and holds six portions. See [[The Cosmic Rings]].

It was once the realm of the six [[The Judgements|Original Judgements]], and each of them held one place. [[The Division]] in 3,097 AF split every Judgement into an archangel and an archdevil, and split each of their places into a heaven and a hell.

## Liminia and the Branches

[[Liminia]] lies at the heart of the realm, and the heavens and hells branch outward from it. Every soul arrives there first.

[[Bis]] decides whether a soul was good or bad on balance, and hates neutrality. [[Seisear]] decides which heaven or hell it goes to.

A heaven and the hell it was split from are still attached to one another, and they are completely divided. They were one place once and the join is still there. Of everything in the realm, each heaven sits literally nearest the hell it was torn from. On the ring of portions the two sit opposite each other, which shows their metaphysical distance. See [[The Cosmic Rings]].

Spiritual nearness runs the other way. Each heaven sits closest in spirit to the hell most like it, and the dead in one are nearer in spirit to the dead in its counterpart than to anyone else.

The hells run in order of severity, from Indulgence at the lightest to Desire at the heaviest, by way of Cruelty, Deceit, Pride and Tyranny.

## The Division and the Tenth

When the Judgements were divided, the archangel and the archdevil of each one fought over the territory they had shared. Most of those quarrels ended in a split of roughly half and half.

[[Tiyanak]] took one tenth and gave [[Verita]] the other nine, willingly. The tenth he kept is the part with [[Indri]] caged in it, and taking the smaller share meant he never had to explain why that piece mattered.

## The Great Separation

In 50 AF the heavens and the hells came apart from one another. See [[Great Separation]].

The souls in them were not moved by it. The archangels and archdevils had to go through the realm by hand, taking souls out of what was no longer theirs and putting them where they now belonged. A few clever souls are thought to have avoided being found during the sorting, and to be where they should not be.

## The Dead

A soul keeps its name and its memory. Both wear down with time, and a soul that has been there long enough may begin to lose the details of who it was.

> *Getting into the afterlives is easy. Everyone manages it eventually, most without meaning to. Getting out again is the part nobody has written a reliable guide to, and I have read all of the unreliable ones.*

## The Living

The living can reach Iudis, and the living have come back out. Leaving is far harder than arriving.

## The Cold War

The [[Divine War]] between the archangels and the archdevils ran from 2,987 AF to 50 AF. It has not resumed. Neither side wants it back, and they take shots at each other from time to time without committing to anything.

## Reach into Arenis

Both sides are worshipped across the mortal world, and worship of the archdevils is as ordinary as worship of the archangels.

The [[Kingdom of Krotaphos]] enforces the will of both in the mortal world, though it is in no position to enforce much at present. It lost its war with the [[Kingdom of Sylfaen]] in 427 AM and has had very little influence since.

## How the Afterlives Work

Each of the twelve afterlives is built around its own domain and handles the souls in it accordingly. A hell is made to punish the kind of person sent to it. A heaven is made to reward the kind of person sent to it.

Underneath the specifics, a heaven is a pleasant place and a hell is not. The hells are cut into rock, and a soul in one spends its time in caverns, tunnels and worked-out stone under a low ceiling. The heavens are open and bright and stand in daylight. The heavens have endless good food and good weather and are full of cheerful lesser angels and other divine company. The hells are too hot, or too cold for anyone who would have preferred the heat, and are crawling with imps and lesser devils.

Most souls get the general treatment of the place they are in. A few get something made for them. A hell will run its ordinary punishment across everyone who arrives and single out particular souls for something specific, built to answer what that soul in particular did.

Three of the hells are run to a plan. In Cruelty, Desire and Tyranny the devils are briefed on each soul, on what it did and how best to torment it. In Indulgence, Deceit and Pride the devils are free to do as they see fit.

None of this makes the archangels good or the archdevils evil. The hells are administered places with institutions, work, standing and economies of their own. A soul can learn a trade there, build a reputation and rise. Rising is genuinely possible, and the punishment does not stop while it happens.

A soul can travel between the heavens and visit any of them. Reward only reaches a soul in the heaven it was assigned to, so visiting is sightseeing.

The hells can be moved between as well. Their borders are policed, crossing is far harder than it is above, and it is hardest of all for a soul bound to a particular hell.

A reward is still a reward, and every heaven carries a cost inside the thing it gives. The cost is never a trick played on the soul. It is what that particular reward turns out to do to a person who receives it without end.

> *I had assumed the hells would be loud. Several of them are. What I had not prepared for was the paperwork. There is a great deal of paperwork. Somewhere in the hells is a soul whose eternal punishment is to be slightly behind on all of it.*

## Design Notes

The two pairings are the whole design. The arrangement that groups each heaven with the hell most like it is the one a visitor feels, and the arrangement that keeps each heaven attached to the hell it was split from is the one that is physically there. A soul has more in common with its spiritual neighbour and shares a wall with its original other half.

The Great Separation is written so that the gods had to do the work themselves, which leaves room for souls in the wrong afterlife and for a DM who wants one.

The heavens are written with an edge for the same reason the archangels are not simply good. A reward that costs nothing gives a campaign nothing to do with it.

The afterlives are built to be livable. A damned soul has somewhere to work and some way to get ahead, which makes a hell a place a campaign can be set in.

Tiyanak taking the smaller share is the Division's quietest move. Every other pair fought for as much as they could get, so a deal that looks like surrender drew no attention at all.
