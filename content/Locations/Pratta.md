The seat of the domain of [[Time]] in [[Iris]], and the home of [[Chronos]]. [[Festivus]] stands inside it.

## The City

Pratta is a mechanism that a visitor walks about inside. Streets are moving parts, running on a cycle. The bridges fold away when they are not scheduled. The doors lock at their appointed times whether or not anyone is through them. None of the works are concealed. A person can stand and watch a quarter of the city change position and will learn nothing from watching it.

Nothing in Pratta is broken. Nothing is late. The maintenance is done out of [[Time|Tráthóirne]] and happens before a fault appears rather than after.

## By Appointment

Nothing here happens unscheduled, visitors included. A newcomer states their business at the edge of the city, is given a time, and is sent to Suanna to wait, which may be a day or a season. The time is kept exactly. Arriving late to it is an offence in this domain, and the consequence for it arrives on schedule as well.

Leaving is also an appointment. A visitor who decides to go early will find that the streets they came in by are not currently in that position, and that the next departure they are down for is the one they booked.

## Chronos

[[Chronos]] is in Pratta and is not difficult to see, provided the appointment exists. The residence sits at the centre of the works. The archfey is built of the same sort of parts the city is, running at all times, hiding nothing.

An agreement made here has already been worked through to its end by the party offering it. That is not a thing the city warns anyone about.

> *My appointment was set for a time eleven days off, which I was told was quick. I spent the eleven days in Suanna, where the beds are good and the food arrives when it says it will. Over those days I became steadily angrier in a way I could not have justified to anybody. Nothing was done to me. I was simply not needed yet, and the city was entirely comfortable with that, and I was not.*

## Design Notes

Pratta is the one city in the setting where a party cannot force the pace. They can fight, bargain, or walk out of anywhere else. Here they wait, and the waiting is the pressure.

The streets moving on a cycle gives a DM a map that changes on a schedule rather than at random, which a clever party can learn and use.

## Open

- How long the city's full cycle runs is unwritten.
- What is done to someone who misses an appointment is unwritten beyond that it is punctual.
- Who sets the schedule, and whether [[Chronos]] can overrule it, is unwritten.
