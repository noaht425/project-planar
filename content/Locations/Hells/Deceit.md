One of the six hells of [[Iudis]], ruled by [[Tiyanak]]. For how punishment and standing work across the afterlives, and how to travel between them, see [[Iudis]].

The caverns are glass, lit in colours, and the walls hand a soul back its own reflection from angles it did not expect. Distance cannot be judged by eye anywhere in it. The whole place is dressed like a fairground.

## The Fairground

Booths line the main galleries, each with a barker and a game. The barkers are imps in painted coats. The games cannot be won, and the barker tells every player that it came close. Prizes sit in glass cases at the back of each booth, in plain view from the gallery, and a soul that wants one will play for years. The lights change colour on a slow cycle, and the shadows they throw change with them.

## What a Soul Is Shown

Deceit is built largely of glass, and most of what happens in it is done to a soul's sight. Souls are shown things that are not there and are given back a view that cannot be trusted. Some are blinded first. A reflection may move slightly late and a doorway may be a painting of one. A soul that calls to someone across a gallery may be calling to its own image, or to a soul in a different gallery whose image has been bent to arrive there.

## The Long Lie

The hell does not rely on single tricks. Each soul is the subject of a lie built over years and told by many voices, usually that a way out has been arranged. The lie is consistent in its details and pays out small confirmations on schedule. A soul that follows it to the end finds that the end has moved, and another lie is already running alongside the first. Small and clumsy lies are looked down on in Deceit, and devils lose standing for telling them. It is not uncommon for a soul in Deceit to be approached by a mysterious stranger who says they have a way out. After years or maybe even decades of putting themself through all sorts of torment to enact this supposed plan, it will eventually be revealed that the plan was a ruse put on by a devil.

## Standing

A soul rises by becoming a good liar. Skilled souls are given booths and then deceptions of their own to run on new arrivals, and rank is judged on the quality of the work. A soul that is running a lie on others still has one running on it.

## Shadows

A shadow in [[Iudis]] belongs to [[Tiyanak]], because none of the other eleven casts one. Deceit is lit from many sides and has more shadows than any other part of the realm. Visitors who try to count them lose track, and the devils decline to say how many are watching.

## The Tenth

The realm of [[Sylas]] was not split evenly. In the deal that ended the [[Divine War]], [[Tiyanak]] gave [[Verita]] ninety per cent of it and kept a tenth, so as to be sure of holding the part where [[Indri]]'s cage lies underground. Deceit is the smallest of the six hells by area, and the glass hides how small it is. Nothing else in the hell was built near it. Since Indri escaped, Tiyanak has had the cage sealed off, and lesser devils are not stationed in that part of the hell.

> *I spent a morning in Deceit walking toward a lamp I could see clearly at the far end of a long gallery. At midday I put out a hand to steady myself and touched it. It had been at my elbow the whole time, and the gallery was a wall.*

## Design Notes

The hell's deceptions are built, which follows Tiyanak's manner. The Long Lie can be run as a subplot that lasts for a whole campaign, with the party never certain which parts of it are the lie.

The penalty for small lies follows Tiyanak's dislike of petty falsehood.

The other five Judgements were split about evenly. Deceit is a tenth of its original domain because Tiyanak gave up the rest to keep the cage.

The shadow section ties the hell to the rule on Tiyanak's page. A party that thinks to look at the floor learns something.
