One of the six hells of [[Iudis]], ruled by [[Belphegor]]. For how punishment and standing work across the afterlives, and how to travel between them, see [[Iudis]].

The caverns are warm and close and the air in them is thick. Feast halls run one into the next, and there is no clean floor anywhere between them.

It ranks as the lightest of the six hells, because no devil in it is assigned to hurt a soul. The souls do that to themselves and to each other, and the hell supplies the occasion without a pause.

## Arrival

A soul entering Indulgence is met in the first cavern by music and a full cup. The welcome is sincere and nobody hurries the arrival. Most souls spend their first hours enjoying themselves, and a fair number decide that the judgement sent them somewhere better than they expected. The belief lasts a few days in some souls and a few years in others. Every soul reaches the same understanding eventually, which is that the party has no edge and no hour at which it ends.

## The Party

No sense is left unoccupied. Bands play in every hall, each in a different key from the next, and singing and shouting run over all of them. The light is amber and unsteady, thrown by lamps and by the molten gold that runs in channels along the walls. [[Belphegor]] and [[Lilien]] consulted each other when they laid out their hells, and both work molten gold into their halls. The air tastes of sugar, spirits, smoke, and sweat. Everything offered to a soul is rich and everything poured for it is strong, and the next cup is in its hand before it has finished the last.

Wherever a soul stands, the others are in sight and in earshot, and the smell reaches it even where they are not. The halls are full of people eating, drinking, vomiting, and having sex, all at once and in plain view, and no soul has ever been offered a quiet room or a door that closes. Souls vomit where they stand and keep drinking. Lesser devils move through the crowd with trays and mops. The floors are mopped continuously and are sticky again within the hour.

## The Rivers

Over-sweetened water and liquor run through the lower caverns in channels wide enough to swim. Souls drown in them regularly, usually while drunk and sometimes while singing. Devils who work the banks pull the drowned out and put a cup in each hand as it wakes, and the soul goes back to the party without being told to.

## Standing

Indulgence has a working economy. The kitchens, breweries, gold works, and cellars all need labour, and a soul that takes work is fed and watered on the same terms as every other soul and is expected to go on enjoying itself between shifts. Standing is measured in halls. A soul that proves reliable is given a hall to keep, which makes it a host, and a host answers for the comfort of everyone in that hall for as long as the hall stands. A host does not sit down while a guest in the hall is still on its feet.

## The Host

[[Belphegor]] attends every hall in turn. He seats guests, asks after their families, remembers the answers, and sends dishes to their places. A soul that refuses is not stopped. It is asked again by a different mouth in slightly different words, until it accepts.

## The Door

Where Indulgence meets [[Temperance]] the join is a single door, and both sides keep it shut. A place is laid at a table in front of it on the Indulgence side. See [[Baator]], whose place this was before the Division.

> *The sweetness is the part nobody warns you about. There is a smell in the lower caverns that I could not get out of my coat for a month, and I burned the coat in the end.*

## Design Notes

Every sense is occupied and no space is private. A party can spend a whole session here without meeting a hostile devil and still find it hard to endure.

Hosts are the hell's ladder. The only way up is to take responsibility for the party itself, which follows the rule on [[Iudis]] that rising is real and the punishment continues while it happens.

The severity ranking from Iudis is kept. The hell is lightest because nothing is done to a soul by design, and the page treats that as a difference in method.
