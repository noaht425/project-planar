One of the six hells of [[Iudis]], ruled by [[Abaddon]]. For how punishment and standing work across the afterlives, and how to travel between them, see [[Iudis]].

The hell is a great many small rooms, each fitted out for its purpose, opening off tunnels that run further than anyone has troubled to map.

Cruelty is torture, administered thoroughly and without ornament. It is the most direct of the hells and makes the least effort to be anything else.

The cruel arrive and become the subjects of their own practice. Souls who were inventive in life are answered inventively.

## The Tunnels

The tunnels are narrow and low, lit by oil lamps hung at long intervals, and the rock between the lamps is dark. Sound does not carry in them. Near Abaddon's seat a cry for help is not heard past sixty feet, and the rest of the hell keeps the same habit. A visitor can walk the length of a tunnel past several hundred occupied rooms and hear nothing but the lamps. Near the seat the walls weep blood that is warm to the touch.

## The Rooms

Each room is fitted for one soul. The tools and the layout are chosen from what the soul did to other people, and most rooms are ready before their occupant arrives. The devils are briefed on each soul before it comes, on what it did and how it is best answered, and they fit a room from the briefing. A soul that arrives is shown to a door with its name already on it. The work in a room is continuous. Attendants change it as the soul adapts to it.

## The Attendants

Lesser devils and imps run the rooms, and they are quiet and exact at it. They do not comment or raise their voices, and they keep written logs of every session.

A soul can rise in Cruelty by earning its way into the work. Souls with standing are trusted with other souls' rooms and apply the methods they used in life, now under supervision. A soul's own room does not close while it works. It keeps its appointments there on a schedule set by the attendants, and the schedule is not shared with the soul.

## Abaddon

Abaddon holds the hell and does not administer it. His seat is at the deepest point, at the end of the tunnel that every other tunnel eventually joins, and he is seldom seen outside it. The attendants run the rooms on routine and refer nothing to him that the routine can answer.

## The Neighbours

Cruelty and [[Kindness]] were one place under [[Hesit]], and where they touch the wall between them is thin. The heaven nearest Cruelty in spirit is [[Sacrifice]], and the dead there are nearer to the souls of Cruelty than to anyone else.

> *I was offered a look inside one of the rooms and declined. On the way out the guide mentioned, as a courtesy, that it had not been one of the occupied ones. It had been fitted out some time ago and was being kept ready, and he understood it was meant for a historian.*

## Design Notes

The quiet tunnels are the main tool. A party hears nothing until a door opens, which gives a DM full control over when a scene starts.

The sixty-foot rule for cries is taken from Abaddon's regional effects and extended here to the whole hell.

The attendants' own rooms apply the rule on [[Iudis]] that rising does not end punishment. Cruelty is the hell where that is stated most plainly.

## Open

- Whether sound passes through the thin wall into Kindness is unsettled. The sixty-foot rule suggests that it does not.
