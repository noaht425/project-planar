Tagra is the market town of the Oinarrian steppe, where the herds and the caravans come in.

## The Market

Tagra is where the steppe sells. Herds come in off the grass, caravans come up from [[Goro]] and across from the Sylfaen border, and the town is arranged around a ground big enough to hold both at once.

It is seasonal, and it is enormous while it is on. For most of the year Tagra is a small place with a very large empty square in it.

## The Weights

The market runs on two sets of weights, one for orcs and one for giants, kept side by side.

Both are real and both are honoured. The conversion between them is published, it is the same for everybody, and a trader may settle in either standard at the other party's choice. Nothing here is ceremonial, and nothing is quietly weighted in one side's favour.

That took a long time. The town will tell a visitor how long, usually at more length than the visitor wanted, and the pride is earned. Two peoples of very different size agreeing on what a fair measure is, and then holding to it for centuries, is not a small administrative achievement. Tagra regards it as the most important thing about itself and is probably right.

The scales are checked in public, on a schedule, by officers drawn from both peoples. A merchant caught on a false weight loses the right to trade on the ground, and the loss is announced.

## The Border

A good deal of what passes through goes on to [[Oinsyl]] and into [[Kingdom of Sylfaen|Sylfaen]]. Tagra's traders deal with Sylfaen's mouth law the way everybody outside Sylfaen deals with it, which is as a tax on time.

So the herding concerns that work both sides of the line mostly keep somebody in Oinsyl whose whole job is paperwork. Tagra finds this funny. Oinsyl does not.

> *The market in Tagra runs on two sets of weights, one for orcs and one for giants. The scales are kept side by side, so nobody ever has to ask which is in use.*

## Design Notes

Tagra is the kingdom at its best, and it is worth having one of those. The two-weights system is the thing a party is told about by a proud local, and it is also genuinely admirable, which makes a change from most of the institutions in this setting.

The empty square out of season is useful. A town built for ten times its present population is a good place for something to happen unobserved.
