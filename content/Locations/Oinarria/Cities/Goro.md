Goro is the capital of [[Kingdom of Oinarria|Oinarria]], built into a cliff above the desert, with the giant halls at the foot of it and the orc houses stacked above. Every public door in the city is two doors, one cut inside the other.

## The Cliff

The giant halls are at ground level, because that is where the height is. Above them the orc quarters are built up the face in storeys, reached by stairs and by ramps wide enough for a giant to climb if a giant has reason to.

Shade is the currency. The afternoon shadow of a wall belongs to whoever built the wall, and that rule is old, written down, and litigated constantly. A strip of shade in Goro is a tenancy.

## The Doors

Every public door is two. A giant's door with an ordinary one cut into it, so that both peoples enter the same building by the same opening, and neither of them has to stoop or climb to do it.

It is a small courtesy and it is the kingdom's whole theory of itself. Visitors are usually shown it within the first hour.

## The Succession

Oinarria has alternated between orc and giantkin rulers since it was founded, and no law requires it. There is no rule of succession that says whose turn it is. It has simply happened that way every time.

So every succession is a live question, and the argument is always the same one. The strongest claim in the room is that it has never yet broken. That is not much of a constitution, and it has held for seven hundred years, which is the point made by everybody who wants it to go on holding.

The crown is claimed through the titans about as often as through descent. A candidate who can show that one of the six has favoured them has an argument that is hard to answer, and the titans are invoked a great deal more often than they are actually consulted.

## The Titans

The titans are worshipped here, more by giantkin than by orcs. To the giantkin they are not figures out of history. Their people had the power from [[Kleipur]] in 1 AM, and they speak of it as recent.

They also turn up. Not often, and not predictably, and a titan in Goro empties a street. The temples keep the arrangements ready at all times for a visit that may not come for a generation.

> *In Goro the afternoon shade belongs to whoever built the wall that casts it. I watched an orc family and a giant family settle an argument over a strip of shadow by measuring it with a rope.*

## Design Notes

The succession is the hook. There is no procedure, only a precedent, so a campaign set near a royal death in Goro is set inside a constitutional crisis that nobody can settle by looking up the rule.

A titan's favour is the kingmaking item, which gives a party something concrete to chase or to prevent.

## Open

- Who sits on the throne now, and which of the two peoples they come from, is unwritten.
- How a claim of a titan's favour is tested, if it is tested at all, is unwritten.
