One of the six heavens of [[Iudis]], ruled by [[Themis]]. For how reward and its cost work across the afterlives, and how to travel between them, see [[Iudis]].

Open plazas under a bright sky, with galleries running around them where the accounting is set out where anyone can walk up and read it.

Every wrong done to a soul in Justice is answered, and the soul is shown the answering.

The accounting runs in both directions. The wrongs the soul itself did are also answered, and it watches those as well.

When the ledger is finally clear there is nothing further to set right, and a soul that was driven by grievance discovers what it had instead of a life.

## The Galleries

The galleries are kept by lesser angels who work as clerks. Each soul has a ledger of two columns, one for what was done to it and one for what it did, and both are posted for anyone to read. The ledgers are updated through the day, and the clerks post the hours at which each column is revised. The answering is shown in the galleries as it happens, in images that stand at the height of a person. The plazas are paved in pale stone and the light off it is strong at midday. Visitors may read any ledger, and the clerks will explain how to find one. Reading another soul's second column is permitted and is considered rude.

## Daily Life

Souls in Justice spend their days at the galleries, and the plazas fill and empty with the revision hours. Arguments over entries are heard by the clerks, who settle them by reference to the record and to nothing else. A soul that thinks an entry is wrong brings evidence. A soul that brings only its feelings is thanked and sent back to the plaza. Few leave angry, because the clerks are patient and the record is hard to fault.

## Themis

[[Themis]] does not hear cases. She reads the rulings of [[Bis]] and sends him objections whenever she disagrees. The clerks copy each objection and post it in the galleries beside the ruling it concerns, so a soul can read both what was decided and what the archangel of justice thought of it.

## The Answering

Where the answering happens depends on the soul that did the wrong. A wrongdoer still alive in [[Arenis]] who is punished there is shown to the wronged soul as the punishment lands, and the soul may call the images up again afterward as often as it likes. A wrongdoer in one of the hells is shown suffering there, and the galleries carry that indefinitely. A wrongdoer in one of the heavens cannot be shown suffering, so the two souls are brought together and left to talk it out.

## Middling Souls

Souls whose two columns run to about the same length have nothing much to watch. They spend their time on each other instead, reading their neighbours' columns and arguing the entries, and the argument is always the same argument: which of them lived the most just life. The clerks do not rule on it. Feuds of this kind have run for centuries and are the main reason a plaza gets loud.

## The Neighbours

Justice and [[Tyranny]] were one place under [[Themati]], and they share walls. [[Indulgence]] is the hell nearest Justice in spirit.

> *The galleries show both columns. Souls arrive to read the column they came for. A few of them stay and read the other one, and the attendants bring those few a chair.*

## Design Notes

The galleries show a ledger of two columns because that is how the heaven works, and the page keeps the author's lines about the ledger exactly as they were.

Posting Themis's objections beside the rulings is an addition. It gives a visiting party a way to see the dispute between Themis and Bis without meeting either.

The feuds give Justice something for a party to walk into. A soul with a grievance against another soul, and no verdict coming, is a job that can be taken up.
