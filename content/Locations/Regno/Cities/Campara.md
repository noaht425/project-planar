Campara (cam-PA-ra) is the market town of the [[Kingdom of Regno|Regno]], inland and well south of the capital.

> *Campara holds its market on a different square each week, by a rota older than anyone selling there, and the town has never once lost track of whose week it is.*

## The Rota

The market moves. Campara has several squares. The market is held in a different one each week, in an order that has not changed in anybody's memory and is not written down anywhere a visitor can consult.

The reason is old and practical. A permanent market makes the ground under it valuable, the families who own that ground rich, and the town's politics a matter of who holds which frontage. Campara spread the market instead. No square is the market, so no family is the market either.

The rota is kept by the town itself rather than by an office. Traders know their week, buyers know where to go, and the few disputes are about which square takes a festival rather than about the order.

## The Trade

Campara sells what the south of Regno grows: grain, fruit, oil, and a great deal of wool. Nothing in it is a luxury trade. The town's advantage is that it sits where the roads from four directions meet, so a farmer with a cart can reach it inside a day.

The money is made on volume and the margins are thin, and that gives the place its character. Campara is busy, unglamorous, and sharper about prices than anywhere else in the kingdom.

## Festivals and the Fifth Square

One square is kept out of the rota and is used for festivals, hangings, proclamations, and the reading of election petitions. It has no market week of its own and nobody has ever proposed giving it one.

## The Council of Squares

The town is run by a council in which every square on the rota has a voice. The traders of each square send one. It is a crowded, argumentative body with a long reputation for getting the roads mended.

No square has ever been given a second voice. Proposals to that effect are what passes for a scandal in Campara.

## Design Notes

The rota is the town in one fact. Regno is a kingdom that does not like concentrated power, and its market town arranged itself the same way centuries before anyone wrote that down as a principle.

A party looking for someone in Campara has to know what week it is, which is a small, pleasant obstacle.
