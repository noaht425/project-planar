Terpisthla (TER-pis-thla), the bright wave, is the port of [[Kingdom of Thalanea|Thalanea]], on the coast facing the sea.

Its harbour is where Thalanea meets the rest of the world, and the city is more relaxed about the fey than anywhere else in the kingdom, which is to say a little.

## The Harbour

Nearly everything Thalanea sells abroad leaves from here, and nearly everything it buys arrives. Cloth, worked stone out of [[Darasil]], and a steady trade in contracts, since a Sylvan agreement drawn up properly is wanted by merchants in several kingdoms who cannot read a word of it.

> *A harbour clerk in Terpisthla stamped my papers, looked at the fae merchant behind me in the queue, and stamped his as well, which I was told afterwards was a very liberal thing to have done.*

## The Open Secret

Thalanea and [[Kingdom of Kumas|Kumas]] trade with each other, steadily and in volume, and both capitals behave as though they do not.

The traffic runs through this harbour and through the border towns. Kumas sends cloth, worked silver, and a good deal of what it buys from [[Iris]]. Thalanea sends stone, grain, and timber. The paperwork describes a great deal of it as coming from somewhere else, and nobody at either end examines the paperwork closely.

Six centuries of cold war have not interrupted it. What the quarrel has produced is an elaborate habit of not mentioning it, which the clerks of Terpisthla maintain better than anyone.

## The Clerks

The harbour office takes a wide view of its own discretion. A clerk who stamps something they were not strictly obliged to stamp is not reported, and the city's position is that the harbour runs because of the latitude rather than in spite of it.

This does not extend to living there. A fae resident in Terpisthla is still remarkable, and the ones who have managed it are mostly married into harbour families.

## Design Notes

The trade is the useful fact. A party told that these two kingdoms are enemies will find a working economy between them within a day of reaching either coast.

The clerks are the people to bribe, flatter, or simply ask, since latitude is already their habit.

## Open

- Whether either crown has ever formally acknowledged the trade is unwritten.
- What proportion of the harbour's paperwork is false is unwritten and is probably large.
