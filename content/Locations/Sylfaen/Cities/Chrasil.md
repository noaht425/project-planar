Name: from [[Dzoa]] chrasa (pine) and ilu (crown), "pine crown".

Chrasil is built in the crowns of the pines, for the Beaked peoples who founded it, and the stairs between its levels were added later for everyone else.

## The Crowns

The pines of Chrasil are old and very tall, and the city sits in the top third of them. Platforms are lashed and pegged rather than nailed, because the trees are alive and still growing, and a rigid joint tears itself out within a decade or so. Bridges run from crown to crown. Nothing in the upper city stays level for long.

The Beaked peoples who founded it did not need stairs. Everybody else does. The stairs came later, which is most of the history of the place in one detail. They are good stairs. They were also an argument that ran for about forty years.

## The Scholars

Chrasil holds the best Dzoa scholars in the kingdom. The grammars are written here, the root lists are kept here, and a question about what a word has always meant is usually settled by somebody in a pine.

This is not the work the Office of the Seal does. The Seal decides what may be said. Chrasil decides what is true about the language, which is a slower business and is not always convenient.

## The Office at the Foot

The Seal keeps its Chrasil offices on the ground, at the foot of the trees. Every Beaked citizen with business there comes down to it.

Both sides read that arrangement as the other's humiliation. The clerks note that the scholars have to descend. The scholars note that the clerks have never once been invited up. Relations are formally excellent. The letters are courteous, the citations are correct, and a scholar asked to appear appears on the day.

What the Seal cannot do is overrule a grammar. So it works around them instead. A ruling cites Chrasil where Chrasil agrees with it, and simply does not mention Chrasil otherwise.

> *The Office of the Seal keeps its offices in Chrasil at the foot of the trees, so that every Beaked citizen with business there has to come down to it.*

## Design Notes

Chrasil is where the mouth law meets the people who actually know the language, and the two do not fit. The Seal needs Chrasil to be right and cannot afford to be seen needing it.

For a party this is the one place in Sylfaen where an official ruling can be argued on the merits rather than appealed. A scholar in a pine can say that a word does not mean what a clerk says it means, and be listened to.
