One of the ten seams of [[Tarkis]], where [[Elemental Plane of Earth|Earth]] and [[Elemental Plane of Air|Air]] meet. For how the realms touch and how the seams work, see [[Tarkis]].

## The Air Itself

There is no water anywhere in the dust seam. Earth's stone comes apart here and Air's wind keeps the pieces up. So the ground is rock and the air above it is rock as well, in grades from gravel down to powder that hangs for days.

Visibility runs from a few hundred feet on a settled day to arm's length in a blow. Everything wears. Rope, cloth, hinges, and lungs all go the same way. The seam's whole economy of maintenance exists because of it.

The dust carries a charge. Hair stands, metal tools spark against stone, and a blow that comes through dry enough will put out a lamp and raise the hair on everyone in a camp.

## The Grades

Dust is sorted and sold by grade, and the sorting is the seam's business.

The coarse grades go to Earth for building. The fine grades go to Fire, where Kalixos works glass and needs abrasive it cannot make for itself, and to Noxia's works. The finest is separated by letting a sealed room stand for a month and taking what is still in the air at the end of it.

A sorter's reputation is her grades, and a camp caught selling one grade as another does not sell again anywhere in Tarkis.

## Working In It

Crews work in short shifts and seal everything. Gear is checked at the start and the end of a shift. A camp will not let a visitor walk out into a blow without someone who knows the ground.

Navigation is by cairn and by count. The cairns are rebuilt constantly. A route is given as a number of paces between them rather than as a direction, since a direction is no use when nobody can see.

## Design Notes

The dust seam supplies the other realms with something they cannot make, which makes it the one seam the five lords have a reason to keep running, and the reason nobody interferes with the sorters.

The charge in the dust gives a DM a reason for magic and metal to behave oddly here without any rule needing to be written for it.

Camps navigate by counted paces, which is a problem a party can be given directly.
