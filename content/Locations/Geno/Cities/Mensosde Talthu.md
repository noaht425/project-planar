Mensosde Talthu (men-SOS-deh TAL-thoo), the hoard of intellect, was founded by followers of [[Mens]], and it is the kingdom's city of scholars.

It is cold and far north, and its libraries are its industry.

## The Toll

The collection is run on Mens's terms. A reader brings something to add to it and is given time with the shelves in proportion to what was brought. Nothing is lent and nothing leaves.

What is added is added permanently. A reader who deposits something for a week of reading has not deposited it for a week. The city is quite clear about this in advance, and it goes on ruining people anyway. Several families in Geno are poorer than their grandparents were because somebody wanted an afternoon with a particular shelf.

## The Assessors

Somebody has to decide what a contribution is worth, and that is the assessors' committee. It sets the exchange between what you brought and the hours you get.

So the committee is the real power in the city, and everybody there knows it. An assessor's favour is worth more than money, since money cannot buy reading time and an assessor can. They are appointed from among the scholars and they are not rotated often.

## Forgeries

Most of the work of the city is authentication. A collection that pays for contributions will be brought fakes, and Mensosde Talthu is the best place in the world to have something checked, because it has had five hundred years of practice at being deceived.

A reader who knowingly brings a forgery is barred for life. The name goes on a list, the list is public, and it is sent to Aravis and to the University at Panthea as a courtesy. There is no appeal and there has never been a pardon.

## Who Does Not Come

Anyone with nothing to bring. There is no fee, no scholarship, and no provision for a reader who is merely good, so the shelves are closed to most of the kingdom as completely as if they were locked.

The scholars here will tell you that is not the city's doing but Mens's. They are mostly right, and it is mostly not an answer.

> *The libraries of Mensosde Talthu lend nothing. A reader brings something to add to the collection and is given time with the shelves in proportion to what was brought. I brought a chapter of this book and was given an afternoon.*

## Design Notes

The city is a lock with a price on it. A party that needs something only this collection holds has to decide what they are willing to give up permanently, which is a better scene than a library check.

The forgery list is also a hook. Somebody on it needs a reader who is not.
