Your patron is [[Archon]], the Archdevil of Tyranny, or [[Themis]], the Archangel of Justice. They were [[Themati]], The King of Kings, whose act was to rule.

In practice that was a working day. Hearings in the morning, petitions after them, and a court that sat whether or not anybody had brought anything to it. Themati wanted the work and was obliged to do it at the same time, so the question of why Themati bothered never came up.

No soul has ever refused a verdict of the six and made the refusal hold.

The Division split the appetite from the obligation. Archon kept the crown and has no hands. Themis kept the scales and has no court. Between the two of them is everything a ruler needs, and neither will ever hold it.

For what applies to all six pacts, including your half and The Offer at 14th level, see [[Warlock Judgements]].

## Level 1 Writ

A writ of the court is a sheet of pressed tin the size of a hand, stamped twice. You can issue one without the tin.

As a bonus action, choose a creature you can see within 60 feet and name one term it may not break. Choose from the following list.

- It may not move further away from you than it already is.
- It may not make an attack roll against any creature other than you.
- It may not cast a spell.

The writ lasts 1 minute, or until you use this feature again. You can have one writ at a time, and you choose a new term each time you issue one.

The first time on each of its turns that the creature breaks the term, it takes damage of your half's type as shown below.

**Archon.** The creature takes damage equal to your Charisma modifier.

**Themis.** The creature takes damage equal to your Charisma modifier plus your proficiency bonus. The term binds you as well, and the first time on each of your turns that you break it, you take the same damage.

## Level 6 Carried Out

Starting at 6th level, you can enforce a ruling rather than price it.

When a creature under your writ breaks its term, you can use your reaction to stop it. The creature makes a Strength saving throw against your warlock spell save DC. On a failed save, the movement or action that broke the term does not happen and is lost.

You can use this feature a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

## Level 10 The Record Office

At 10th level, you can have two writs at a time, each with its own term, and you gain the following benefit according to your half.

**Archon.** When a creature under your writ drops to 0 hit points, you can issue a new writ with no action required.

**Themis.** A creature under your writ has disadvantage on saving throws against your spells, for as long as you have not broken that writ's term yourself.

## Design Notes

A writ with a named term is the shape of this pair. Archon holds the appetite and Themis the obligation, so the same writ costs the tyrant nothing and binds the judge.

Themis is the better half at a table that can keep its own rules. Nowhere else in the six is an angel half mechanically stronger than its devil. The extra damage and the 10th-level disadvantage both switch off the moment the warlock breaks its own writ.

Three terms rather than a free-text restriction. A term the player writes would land the DM with adjudication every round, and a court that ran on three standing orders is closer to how Themati worked anyway.

The record office is in the feature name and nothing else. Clerks who answer to nobody are a resource for a party rather than a power a warlock carries.

## Open

- Whether a Themis warlock who breaks a writ owes anything beyond the damage is unwritten. An archangel of justice would have views about a judge in contempt.
- Whether [[Archon]] grants this pact at all to someone who will not use it. He has worked at being necessary for nine thousand years and has never managed it.
