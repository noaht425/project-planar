## Design notes

The archdevils and archangels are the largest hole. Twelve patrons have finished pages and no mechanics, and they are paired: every archdevil was half of a Judgement whose other half is an archangel still walking around. Writing the two subclasses separately would waste that, since the interesting version of either one is the half that is missing.

The ascendant gods are the largest group by count and the least suited to one subclass. Eighteen individuals with nothing in common except having been mortal means either one flexible subclass with a choice table, in the manner of [[Sorcerer Devil]], or a subclass per god as each becomes a patron in play. Ambition was written the second way because [[Ignatius]] took a live character.

## Open

A full custom class called the Soulbroker exists outside this vault, built on the 2014 rules. Constitution casts, pact-style slots recharge on a short rest, and one resource called Souls pays for ghouls, grasping hands, and a gravestone. Its three subclasses answer where the power came from: a patron, private study, or the dead speaking through you.

It was designed without Agra in mind and it does not fit yet. It assumes souls are loose things a person can catch. Here they go to [[Iudis]], get judged, and get housed, and the archdevils and archangels run that process. A Soulbroker is interfering with an institution rather than working an empty field.

Four things to settle before it enters the setting.

- Who objects to a soul being caught before it reaches [[Iudis]]. [[Abaddon]] of Cruelty and [[Charis]], who hoards the dead, both have standing.
- Whether the patron subclass belongs to an existing figure. [[Charis]] already collects the dead. The subclass is currently written for a generic lich, which this setting does not need.
- Whether the subclass that speaks with the dead overlaps the work of the [[The University of Divine Judgement]].
- Whether a Soulbroker is a class or a calling. [[300 What You Do]] sorts classes by whether their power has an owner. A Soulbroker draws on the dead themselves, which is a sixth answer that page does not offer.
