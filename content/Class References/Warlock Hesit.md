Your patron is [[Abaddon]], the Archdevil of Cruelty, or [[Neema]], the Archangel of Kindness. They were [[Hesit]], The Empath, whose act was to feel what another feels.

The feeling came whole and came uninvited. Hesit had no say in whose life arrived or when, and no way to set one down once it had started. Whoever stood in front of Hesit saw their own face looking back, and whatever had been done to that person showed on Hesit's body until the life was finished.

The Division split that in two. Neema kept the face and shows a petitioner their own better self. Abaddon kept the marks, and the swords and spears standing in him belong to other people. Not one of them has ever been pulled out.

For what applies to all six pacts, including your half and The Offer at 14th level, see [[Warlock Judgements]].

## Level 1 Felt Through

You feel what another feels, in the manner of the thing that made you, which is to say without being asked and without much mercy.

As a bonus action, choose a creature you can see within 60 feet. You learn its current hit points, any conditions affecting it, and the one thing it most wants at this moment. Your DM gives you that last part in a sentence.

The creature knows you have done it. There was never a wall in Hesit's place and there is none here.

## Level 6 Borrowed Faces

Starting at 6th level, you can finish the half of the act your patron cannot, in the way your patron cannot do it.

**Abaddon.** When a creature you can see within 30 feet takes damage, you can use your reaction to take that damage instead of it. The damage cannot be reduced or prevented, and hit points lost this way cannot be restored until you finish a long rest.

While you are carrying damage taken this way, you deal an extra 1d6 necrotic damage once on each of your turns, and you have advantage on saving throws against being frightened or charmed.

**Neema.** When a creature that can see you within 30 feet makes an attack roll, a saving throw, or an ability check, you can use your reaction to show it the version of itself it is not. It rolls with advantage, and it gains temporary hit points equal to your warlock level.

A creature cannot be shown this twice before it finishes a long rest.

Either way, you can use this feature a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

## Level 10 The Thin Wall

At 10th level, nothing within reach of you can be kept from you, because the idea of keeping it has not been provided for.

You can use Felt Through on any number of creatures within 60 feet with the same bonus action.

You also gain the following, according to your half.

**Abaddon.** A creature you have used Felt Through on this turn has disadvantage on the next saving throw it makes against a spell you cast before the end of your next turn.

**Neema.** A creature you have used Felt Through on cannot lie to you until the end of your next turn. It knows that it cannot, which is usually worse.

## Design Notes

Hesit's two halves are the furthest apart of the six in what they do with the same gift, so this is the Judgement where the table does the most work. Abaddon's version moves harm onto the warlock permanently. Neema's hands out advantage and temporary hit points. Both come from knowing exactly how somebody else feels.

Hit points lost to Abaddon's half not returning until a long rest is the mark staying in. It is a real cost and it is meant to accumulate across a day, in the way the weapons standing in him have accumulated across nine thousand years.

Felt Through announcing itself is the thin wall. A warlock of this Judgement cannot read a room quietly, and that is the drawback the feature carries instead of a use limit.

The backlog did not make it into any feature. Feeling a life in real time is a thing a Judgement could do and a warlock cannot, so it stays fiction.

## Open

- Whether Abaddon's half can take damage on behalf of an unwilling creature is unsettled. Taking a blow off an enemy to deny them a death is a cruelty of the right shape.
- Whether Neema will let a warlock use this on somebody Neema did not send them to is open, and it is the sort of thing Neema would have opinions about without stating them.
