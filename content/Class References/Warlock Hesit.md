Your patron is [[Abaddon]], the Archdevil of Cruelty, or [[Neema]], the Archangel of Kindness. They were [[Hesit]], The Empath, whose act was to feel what another feels.

The feeling came whole and came uninvited. Hesit had no say in whose life arrived or when, and no way to set one down once it had started. Whoever stood in front of Hesit saw their own face looking back, and whatever had been done to that person showed on Hesit's body until the life was finished.

The Division split that in two. Neema kept the face and shows a petitioner their own better self. Abaddon kept the marks, and the swords and spears standing in him belong to other people. Not one of them has ever been pulled out.

For what applies to all six pacts, including your half and The Offer at 14th level, see [[Warlock Judgements]].

## Level 1 Felt Through

What another creature feels arrives in you whole.

As a bonus action, choose a creature you can see within 60 feet. You learn its current hit points, any conditions affecting it, and the one thing it most wants at this moment. Your DM gives you that last part in a sentence.

The creature knows you have used this feature on it.

## Level 6 Borrowed Faces

Starting at 6th level, you can take the part of the act your patron did not keep.

**Abaddon.** When a creature you can see within 30 feet takes damage, you can use your reaction to take that damage instead of that creature. The damage cannot be reduced or prevented, and hit points you lose this way cannot be restored until you finish a long rest.

Until you finish a long rest after using this feature, you deal an extra 1d6 necrotic damage once on each of your turns, and you have advantage on saving throws against being charmed or frightened.

**Neema.** When a creature that can see you within 30 feet makes an attack roll, a saving throw, or an ability check, you can use your reaction to give it advantage on that roll. It also gains temporary hit points equal to your warlock level, and the next time it hits a creature with an attack before the end of its next turn, that attack deals extra radiant damage equal to your Charisma modifier.

A creature cannot gain this benefit again until it finishes a long rest.

You can use this feature a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

## Level 10 The Thin Wall

At 10th level, nothing near you is kept from you.

You can use Felt Through on any number of creatures within 60 feet with the same bonus action.

You also gain the following benefit, according to your half.

**Abaddon.** A creature you have used Felt Through on has disadvantage on the next saving throw it makes against a spell you cast before the end of your next turn.

**Neema.** A creature you have used Felt Through on cannot knowingly speak a lie to you until the end of your next turn. It is aware of this effect.

## Design Notes

Hesit's two halves are the furthest apart of the six in what they do with the same gift, so the table carries more weight here than it will elsewhere. Abaddon's version moves harm onto the warlock and leaves it there. Neema's hands out advantage and temporary hit points. Both run on knowing exactly how somebody else feels.

Hit points lost to Abaddon's half not returning until a long rest is the mark staying in. The cost is meant to accumulate across a day of play.

Felt Through announcing itself is the thin wall. A warlock of this Judgement cannot read a room quietly, and that stands in place of a use limit on the feature.

The backlog is in no feature. Feeling a life in real time is a thing a Judgement could do and a warlock cannot, so it stays fiction.

## Open

- Whether Abaddon's half can take damage on behalf of an unwilling creature is unsettled. Taking a blow off an enemy to deny them a death is a cruelty of the right shape.
- Whether Neema will let a warlock use this on somebody Neema did not send them to. Neema has stated no position.
