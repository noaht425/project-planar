Your patron is [[Lilien]], the Archdevil of Desire, or [[Gwenhael]], the Archangel of Sacrifice. They were [[Aaranea]], The Lover, whose act was to love.

In Aaranea that was one motion with two ends, wanting a thing and handing it over for the sake of the thing wanted, and nobody inside the act could say where the first of those stopped.

Aaranea's part in a hearing was to argue for the soul. It worked from what the soul had wanted, put that to the other five, and paid for the argument on the spot, in front of the panel and in front of the soul, giving up something of its own every time it spoke. It lost more often than it won. It argued the next hearing anyway, for seven thousand years.

The Division split the wanting from the giving. Lilien is the only one of the twelve who cannot hide being two. What is left of Gwenhael is a thin man with both arms busy covering himself.

Nobody holds Aaranea's part now. [[Bis]] and [[Seisear]] hear no representations, so no soul arriving in [[Liminia]] since 50 AF has had anyone speak for it.

For what applies to all six pacts, including your half and The Offer at 14th level, see [[Warlock Judgements]].

## Level 1 Paid on the Spot

You can give up something of your own on another creature's behalf. The price comes due as you speak and it is paid in the open.

Once on each of your turns, you can lose hit points equal to your warlock level. This loss cannot be reduced or prevented. Choose a creature other than you that you can see within 60 feet, and give it the benefit of your half.

**Lilien.** The creature has advantage on its next attack roll made before the end of its next turn. The first time it hits with that attack, the damage increases by your Charisma modifier.

**Gwenhael.** The creature gains temporary hit points equal to twice your warlock level.

## Level 6 Argued Anyway

Starting at 6th level, losing is not a reason to stop.

When a creature you paid for misses with the attack, fails the saving throw it was about to make, or drops to 0 hit points before the end of your next turn, you can use Paid on the Spot again on the same turn. Doing so costs you the hit points again.

You can use this feature a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

## Level 10 Nobody Else Will

At 10th level, you can speak for the dying.

When a creature you can see within 60 feet drops to 0 hit points, you can use your reaction to pay. You lose hit points equal to twice your warlock level, and the creature drops to 1 hit point instead of 0.

You also gain the following benefit according to your half.

**Lilien.** The creature has advantage on attack rolls until the end of its next turn.

**Gwenhael.** The creature regains hit points equal to the hit points you lost instead of dropping to 1.

Once you use this feature, you cannot use it again until you finish a long rest.

## Design Notes

Every feature on this page costs the warlock hit points at the moment it is used. That is the one thing Aaranea did that none of the other five did. It is why this pair is written as a support subclass that bleeds for its party.

Paying before the roll resolves is deliberate. Aaranea paid in front of the panel and then lost most of the time, so a player who pays and watches the attack miss has had the authentic experience. The 6th-level feature is what Aaranea did about that, and it costs again.

The 10th-level feature fills an office that has stood vacant since 50 AF. A warlock doing it is doing something the realm has no procedure for, and that is worth a conversation with a Nekerux rather than a mechanical penalty.

Lilien and Gwenhael both suffer visibly, so neither half is the comfortable pick. Lilien's riders are offensive and Gwenhael's are protective, and both are paid for out of the same pool.
