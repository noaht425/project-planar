Shared guidance for both books. Anything that applies to more than one page belongs here rather than being repeated on each page.

There are two books and they have different readers.

The **sourcebook** is for the DM. It is written by the historian described below and it contains everything, including what the players must not know.

The **Player's Guide** is for players. It is written in the author's own voice, speaking directly to them, casual and funny. The historian appears in it only inside flavor boxes. Nothing in it may contain material the players should not have.

The two are separated by audience rather than by site folder. Every note is DM-only by default. A note reaches the player site only if its frontmatter contains `audience: player`, so forgetting to mark something hides it rather than exposing it.

The Player's Guide pages and the class references carry that tag. Nothing else does yet.

In the player build, a link pointing at a note that was not included is flattened to plain text, so the player site has no dangling links and no leaked note titles.

## The Narrator

One person writes the whole book. They are a historian who travels, and they make a point of meeting people rather than working only from documents. They use records as well, and the two sources are not always in agreement.

They are not named and no gender is given. Everything they write is in the first person.

They have been made to learn the languages and resent it in a way that is visible on the page. The language chapters are the same person as the travel chapters, further into the work and less delighted about it.

What they have reached shapes what they can say. They have met archfey, dragons, people in all five elemental realms, and some of the archangels and archdevils. They have not met the Kings. See the reachability ladder in [[Arenis]].

The voice is thorough and truthful, with jokes in it. They do not editorialise about how clever something is and they do not tell the reader how to feel.

## The Two Layers

Every page carries the in-world text and the design reasoning, kept apart.

The body is the in-world account, written flat and declarative in the style of a published rules text. Flavor goes in italic quote boxes in the narrator's voice.

Design notes sit in their own section at the foot of the page and are written out of character. They record why a choice was made, not what the thing is. Anything the author believes but has not settled as canon goes here rather than in the body.

Unresolved material goes in an Open section below the design notes, so a gap is visible rather than quietly filled.

## Flavor Boxes

One per page on anything substantial: languages, significant people, places, items, events.

A box is grounded in the specific mechanic of its subject. A box that would work equally well on three different pages is not finished.

Boxes do not repeat what the body already said.

## Pronunciations

A name is respelled the first time it appears on a page, in parentheses after it, with the stressed syllable capitalised.

Respellings are derived from the language's own sound rules rather than invented. High Fae stresses the first syllable, Ancient Draconic the penultimate.

Two names in the same language should not respell identically. Where that happens, one of the names is changed.

## Shared Guidance

A rule that governs a group of pages is written once on the parent page and linked from the others. The afterlives work this way, with the common rules on [[Iudis]] and only the specifics on each of the twelve.

## The Player's Guide

The guide is a path. Each page ends in choices that route the reader onward, and the spine is four decisions: what you are, where you are from, what you do, who you owe.

Pages are numbered in blocks with gaps, so inserting a page never renumbers another one. The number is part of the title and never changes.

| Block | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 99 | Opening, the world in brief, how to use the guide |
| 100s | The nine peoples |
| 200s | Homelands |
| 300s | Callings |
| 400s | Patrons, in two layers: a group page, then one page per being |
| 900 | Finish, and what the DM needs |

Patrons go through their group first. A calling page sends the reader to the group (the Archfey, the Archdevils, the Dragons and so on), and the group page sends them to the individual. No calling page links straight to a single patron.

The path is a graph rather than a tree. A later page may hand the reader something an earlier page did not offer, and when it does it says so under a heading of its own and links to it. A page reachable only that way says so at the top.

Branches that have not been written yet say so on the page and invite the reader to ask for them, rather than linking nowhere.
