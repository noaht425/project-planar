Read [[Sourcebook Conventions]] first. This note is the long form of its Flavor Boxes section and does not replace it.

## The Job

Audit every flavor box in the vault, rewrite the ones that are not working, and leave the ones that are.

As of this writing there are 45 boxes across the archdevils, archangels, archfey, wind gods, Beings of the Eternal Void, dragons and Nekerukes, plus the Dragons and Nekerux faction notes. They were written in one long session and they have one writer's tic repeated through them. The problem is the sameness rather than the individual boxes, so this is a pass over the whole set rather than a hunt for bad ones.

## Where Things Are

The vault is the source of truth. It is a personal app similar to Obsidian, using `[[ ]]` links. The author is not using Obsidian.

Boxes live in notes under `NPCs/` and `Factions/`. The format is one markdown blockquote in italics, on its own line:

```
> *Text of the box.*
```

One per page on anything substantial. Plane and realm notes under `Locations/` have their own boxes and are in scope too, though they were written earlier and more slowly and are generally in better shape.

## The Narrator

One person writes the whole sourcebook. Everything in a box is first person, in that person's voice.

They are a historian who travels. They work from records as well, and the two sources are not always in agreement. They make a point of meeting people rather than only reading about them.

They are not named and no gender is given. Keep it that way.

They have been made to learn the languages of the setting and resent it visibly. The language chapters are the same person further into the work and less delighted about it.

What they have reached shapes what they can say. They have met archfey, dragons, people in all five elemental realms, and some of the archangels and archdevils. They have not met the Kings of day and night. A box about something out of reach has to be built from records or from somebody else's testimony, and should say so.

They are thorough and truthful, with jokes in them. They do not editorialise about how clever a thing is and they do not tell the reader how to feel.

## House Style

These are standing preferences for the whole project and they apply inside boxes.

- No em dashes.
- No "not X, but Y" constructions.
- No three-item rhythms. Two items or four.
- No portentous one-line section closers.
- Nothing that tells the reader a rule or a design is clever or elegant.
- Flat declarative prose. The flavor is in the observation rather than in the adjectives.
- Gender-neutral language where a gender is not established.
- Nothing that reads as AI-written. Repeated sentence shapes across many entries is the main way that happens, which is exactly what went wrong here.

## What Is Wrong With The Current Set

Counted across the 45 boxes:

| Habit | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Contains "I asked" | 15 |
| Opens with "I asked" | 9 |
| Ends on the narrator's unresolved verdict | 9 |
| Built from records rather than an encounter | 6 |
| Hinges on a specific count | 21 |

The number words alone: "four" nine times, "nine" six times, "eleven" six times. Two separate boxes have the narrator staying somewhere for eleven days.

Every box is between 45 and 79 words and most are between 53 and 70. There is no short sharp one anywhere in the set and no long one.

The dominant shape is: the narrator asks a question, gets a refusal or a reversal, and closes by reporting that they have not worked out how they feel about it. Boxes ending that way include Blythe, Brilis, Gwenhael, Lilien, Mens, Notus, Pavlas, Bis and Nekerux. Any one of them is fine. Nine of them is a signature.

Specific weak ones to deal with first:

- **Boreas.** The joke about the weather lands and the box is about his court instead of about him. His betrothal to [[Princess Verna]] is the whole character and the box does not touch it.
- **Charis.** Exposition. It restates how Rot works, restates how Charis works, then delivers a flat line. There is no scene in it.
- **Princess Esta.** The first half repeats the Murlanna material from [[Seasons]] almost directly. Boxes are not allowed to repeat the body.
- **Zephyros.** Serviceable, and its "eleven days" duplicates Tenu's.
- **Belphegor.** The final clause explains the joke that the preceding sentence already made.

## The Standard

These are the ones to write toward. What they have in common is that they report a concrete thing the narrator observed which the body of the page does not contain, and the meaning arrives through that detail rather than through the narrator announcing what it meant.

> *The gauntlets are the thing nobody mentions. He has not touched anything with his own hands since the Division. Every cup he has lifted, he has lifted with those on, and I watched him pick up a document and tear it without meaning to, and then apologise to me, and then do it again with the next one.*

> *I was allowed in and shown a shelf. On it were eleven objects, all of them ugly, none of them worth anything to anyone outside that mountain. She named what each one had survived. The eleventh was a clay cup and she had been keeping it for nine thousand years because it had come through a thing that had taken a city.*

> *The ledger is the hoard, which I understood as a figure of speech until I was shown it. It is a physical thing, it is enormous, and there is a department. I asked whether a debt is ever written off. The clerk did not understand the question, and when I rephrased it she told me that a debt that was written off would have to come out of the hoard.*

> *Fifty years of storm and the air realm still talks about him, and the talk is not reverent. It is specific. A bridge he paid for. A tax he refused to allow. A district he had rebuilt twice because he did not like how the second one looked. I have not heard any other dragon spoken about that way, and I have met the rest of them.*

Tests a box has to pass:

1. **It is grounded in the specific mechanic of its subject.** A box that would work equally well on three other pages is not finished.
2. **It adds something.** If the body already said it, the box is wasted.
3. **It shows rather than concludes.** The torn document is the character. Nobody has to say what it means.
4. **The narrator is in it as a person.** They were somewhere, they did something, it went a particular way.
5. **It would be cut from a published sourcebook if it were only atmosphere.** Every box should leave a DM with something usable, an image or a fact or a way the thing behaves.

## Variety Budget

Across any group of six or more boxes:

- At most one may open with "I asked".
- At most one may end on the narrator's unresolved feeling about what happened.
- At most two may hinge on a counted quantity, and the counts must not repeat across the whole book.
- At most one may be built from documents rather than from an encounter, unless the subject is genuinely out of the narrator's reach.

Length should range. Some boxes should be two sentences and some should run to eight. A twenty-word box is allowed and there is currently not a single one.

Other shapes that are available and barely used in the current set:

- Somebody else's testimony, reported. The Abaddon box does this and it is the only one.
- The narrator being wrong about something and saying so.
- The narrator's own irritation, particularly anywhere a language is involved.
- A thing the narrator was shown without explanation and still cannot account for.
- A practical detail from an ordinary person who works there. The Autuun box does this with the foreman and it is one of the better ones.
- The narrator declining to write something down.

## How To Work

Go group by group. After each group, re-read all of that group's boxes in sequence and check them against the variety budget. The failure happens when boxes are written one page at a time and never read side by side.

Change only the blockquote line. Do not touch the body, the design notes, the frontmatter or the stat blocks.

Some boxes have already been edited by the author by hand. Treat anything that reads well as deliberate and leave it. If a box seems weak but may be the author's own writing, raise it rather than overwriting it.

Pronunciation respellings appear on first occurrence of a name on a page. If a rewrite moves a name's first occurrence into or out of a box, the respelling moves with it. Never put a respelling inside frontmatter.

After each group, check for broken `[[ ]]` links, duplicate respellings across the set, stray working notes left in prose, and doubled parentheticals. The previous session's check script covers all four.
